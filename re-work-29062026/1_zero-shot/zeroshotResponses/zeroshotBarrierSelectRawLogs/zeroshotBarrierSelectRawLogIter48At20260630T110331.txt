Based on the given context, here are five barriers that I would consider for the Shift2DC project:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: Direct current (DC) systems have the potential to reduce power losses compared to alternating current (AC), but there can still be challenges related to quality, safety, and system integrity.

2. Reduced reliability in DC devices: Some concerns exist about the reliability of DC components, as they may not be as well-established or mature as AC alternatives. This could lead to increased maintenance requirements and downtime.

3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: Demonstrating clear advantages for DC technologies in various applications is essential for widespread adoption. Identifying sectors like ports, industry, data centers, and buildings where DC can offer substantial benefits will help drive the transition.

4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): Establishing clear guidelines and agreements between utilities and DC system owners is crucial for ensuring efficient net metering, preventing disputes over ownership, and developing consistent industry standards.

5. Lack of pilot projects: Demonstrating the successful implementation of DC technologies through pilot projects can help build confidence in the technology among stakeholders, paving the way for broader adoption.

These five barriers are important considerations for the Shift2DC project as they directly impact the development and deployment of DC solutions in various sectors. Overcoming these challenges will be essential for accelerating the transition to a sustainable energy system using direct current technologies.
