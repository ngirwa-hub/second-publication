These five barriers have been selected as they present significant challenges in the transition to a sustainable energy system using direct current (DC) technologies:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: The conversion from AC to DC and back again can result in power losses and potential safety concerns, which can impact the overall efficiency and cost-effectiveness of DC solutions.
2. Uncertain utility interaction: Clear guidelines and standards for net metering, utility ownership, and agreed protocols are necessary to ensure seamless integration between DC systems and existing energy infrastructure.
3. Lack of pilot projects: Demonstrating the practicality and effectiveness of DC technologies through pilot projects is crucial in gaining public trust and promoting widespread adoption.
4. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Developing standardized components that can work together seamlessly will help reduce complexity, cost, and potential safety risks associated with using multiple incompatible DC devices.
5. High costs of DC solutions: Reducing the financial burden on consumers and businesses is essential for widespread adoption of DC technologies, as high upfront costs may deter potential adopters.
