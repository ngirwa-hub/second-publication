--- mistral_subject-matter ---
1. power losses, quality and safety issues
2. reduced reliability in DC devices
3. uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards)
4. lack of pilot projects
5. uncertain regulatory roadmap

These five barriers were selected as they directly impact the adoption and implementation of Direct Current (DC) technologies in various sectors. Power losses, quality, and safety issues are significant concerns in DC systems that need to be addressed for efficient energy transmission. Reduced reliability in DC devices can lead to system failures and increased maintenance costs. Uncertain utility interaction can create barriers for net metering, utility ownership, and the adoption of agreed standards, which are essential for the successful integration of DC technologies. Lack of pilot projects hinders the demonstration of the benefits of DC technologies and their potential for widespread adoption. Lastly, an uncertain regulatory roadmap can create uncertainty for investors and project developers, making it difficult to plan and implement DC projects.
