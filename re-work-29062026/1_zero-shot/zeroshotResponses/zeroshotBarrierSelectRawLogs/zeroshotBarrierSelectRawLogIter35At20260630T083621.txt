This could involve showcasing successful pilot projects, demonstrating cost savings, or highlighting environmental benefits.

3. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): Clear guidelines and agreements between utilities and DC technology developers are necessary for the smooth integration of these technologies into the existing energy infrastructure. This will help ensure that both parties can operate efficiently and with minimal disruption.

4. Lack of pilot projects: Real-world demonstrations of DC technologies in various sectors (ports, industry, data centers, and buildings) are essential to validate their performance, gather feedback, and build confidence among stakeholders.

5. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: A clear and supportive regulatory environment is necessary for the successful deployment of DC technologies. This includes policies that encourage investment, provide incentives for renewable energy adoption, and ensure compliance with safety and environmental regulations.
