---

In the context of the Shift2DC project, which focuses on advancing medium- and low-voltage DC technologies, the Static protection system is rated as "Very important" due to its essential role in ensuring grid stability and protecting infrastructure. DC systems do not have natural current zero-crossing points like AC systems, making fault management more challenging. Thus, advanced fault detection and ultra-fast isolation capabilities are crucial for preventing potential damage or outages caused by faults within the system.

Moreover, as DC technologies become more integrated into various applications such as data centers, buildings, industrial settings, and ports, the complexity of managing these microgrids increases. The Static protection system's ability to rapidly identify and isolate faults is vital for maintaining uninterrupted operation, particularly when renewable energy sources are involved, which can introduce variability in power generation.

Additionally, the safety aspect cannot be overstated; DC systems operating at higher voltages can pose significant risks if not properly managed. The inclusion of ultra-fast protection devices within a Static protection system ensures that any faults are quickly addressed, minimizing the risk to both equipment and personnel.

Given these considerations, alongside the project's aim to establish guidelines and roadmaps for widespread DC adoption, ensuring robust fault management systems like the Static protection system are in place is paramount. It not only supports technical reliability but also builds confidence among potential adopters regarding the safety and feasibility of integrating DC solutions into their operations. Therefore, its importance within the Shift2DC project's scope—aiming to facilitate the broader acceptance and implementation of DC technologies—is deemed very high.

The ability to quickly detect and isolate faults enhances system resilience, reduces downtime, and minimizes the risk of cascading failures—a crucial consideration as these systems become more prevalent in diverse applications such as data centers, buildings, industry, and ports.
