Based on my expertise, I would select the following five barriers for medium- and low-voltage DC technologies:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues (Barrier #1) - The conversion between AC and DC can result in energy losses, which could impact overall efficiency. Ensuring quality and safety standards are met is crucial to address these concerns.

2. Reduced reliability in DC devices (Barrier #3) - Compared to their AC counterparts, DC components may have lower reliability due to a limited range of commercially available parts and components. Improving the reliability of DC devices is essential for widespread adoption.

3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous (Barrier #9) - Demonstrating clear advantages of DC technology over AC systems is necessary to encourage greater adoption. Identifying specific applications where DC offers significant benefits can help drive growth in the market.

4. Uncertain utility interaction (Barrier #4) - Clear guidelines and agreements between utilities, users, and regulators are needed for issues such as net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards. Addressing these uncertainties is crucial to ensure a smooth transition towards DC technology.

5. Lack of pilot projects (Barrier #10) - Real-world demonstrations and case studies showcasing the benefits and feasibility of DC systems are essential for building confidence in the technology among stakeholders, investors, and end-users. More pilot projects can help accelerate the adoption of DC technologies.
