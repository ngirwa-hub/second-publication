Power losses, quality, and safety issues: The use of direct current (DC) technology can lead to reduced power losses, but there are still concerns about the quality and safety of DC systems compared to alternating current (AC) systems. Ensuring proper insulation, isolation, and protection mechanisms is crucial in overcoming these challenges.

2. Reduced reliability in DC devices: While some DC components have shown high reliability, there remains a general concern that the reliability of DC devices may be lower than AC devices due to factors like limited availability and maturity of technologies. Improving the design and manufacturing processes for DC components can help mitigate this issue.

3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: One of the main challenges faced by DC technology is demonstrating its clear advantages over AC systems, particularly in terms of power transmission efficiency and system simplicity. Developing more compelling use-cases that highlight these benefits can help drive broader adoption of DC technologies.

4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): The integration of renewable energy sources into the grid requires clear regulations regarding net metering, utility ownership, and standardized protocols for interfacing with AC grids. Establishing a supportive regulatory framework that encourages the use of DC technologies is essential to their widespread adoption.

5. Lack of pilot projects: To demonstrate the viability and benefits of DC systems, more pilot projects are needed that showcase their capabilities in real-world applications. These demonstrations can help build confidence in the technology among stakeholders, including utilities, regulators, and end users.
