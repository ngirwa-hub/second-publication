--- generalist_mistral ---
Based on my role as an expert in renewable energy and direct current (DC) technologies, I would select the following five barriers to the widespread adoption of medium- and low-voltage DC:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: The conversion between AC and DC can lead to power loss and potential safety hazards. Addressing these concerns is crucial for ensuring the efficient and safe operation of DC systems.
2. Reduced reliability in DC devices: Some DC components may have lower reliability compared to their AC counterparts, which could increase maintenance costs and decrease system uptime. Improving the reliability of DC devices will be essential for widespread adoption.
3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: Demonstrating the benefits of using DC over AC in specific applications is crucial to encourage its adoption. Identifying and showcasing successful applications can help build momentum for wider implementation.
4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): Clear guidelines and regulations regarding net metering, utility ownership, and standards are necessary to ensure a smooth transition to DC systems and to facilitate grid integration.
5. Lack of pilot projects: Real-world demonstrations of DC technologies can help build confidence and address any technical or operational uncertainties. The development of pilot projects is essential for gathering data on performance, cost-effectiveness, and overall feasibility.
