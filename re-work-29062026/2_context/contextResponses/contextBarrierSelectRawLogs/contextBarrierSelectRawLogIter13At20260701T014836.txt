--- generalist_mistral ---
Based on my role as an expert in renewable energy and direct current (DC) technologies, I would select the following five barriers for consideration:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: The higher efficiency of DC systems compared to AC is a significant advantage, but concerns about power loss, quality, and safety persist due to the need for proper design, installation, and maintenance of DC components.
2. Reduced reliability in DC devices: Although advancements have been made in DC technologies, some challenges remain with regards to the reliability and longevity of DC devices compared to their AC counterparts, which may impact the overall system performance and lifespan.
3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: The widespread adoption of DC systems requires demonstrable advantages over traditional AC systems, particularly in terms of efficiency, cost, and ease of integration with renewables. More compelling use cases are needed to drive wider acceptance and investment in DC technologies.
4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): The interplay between DC systems and utilities is complex, with uncertainties surrounding net metering policies, utility ownership of DC infrastructure, and the need for standardization to ensure seamless integration into existing grid structures.
5. Lack of pilot projects: Real-world demonstrations and pilot projects are crucial for validating the performance, reliability, and cost-effectiveness of DC technologies. More pilot projects can help build confidence in the technology among stakeholders and facilitate its broader adoption.
