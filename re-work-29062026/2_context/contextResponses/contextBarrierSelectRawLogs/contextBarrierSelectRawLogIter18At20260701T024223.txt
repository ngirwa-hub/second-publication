--- generalist_mistral ---
Based on my expert role, I would select the following five barriers:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: The use of direct current (DC) can lead to reduced power loss compared to alternating current (AC), but there are still concerns about maintaining quality and ensuring safety in DC systems.
2. Reduced reliability in DC devices: While AC-to-DC and DC-to-AC converters have become more reliable, there is still a concern that the overall system might be less reliable due to the additional components involved in DC systems.
3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: There are limited applications where the advantages of using DC over AC outweigh the costs and complexity. More demonstration projects are needed to showcase these advantages.
4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): The integration of DC systems with existing grid infrastructure requires clear rules and agreements regarding net metering, utility ownership, and standardized protocols for communication and interoperability between devices.
5. Lack of pilot projects: More demonstration projects are needed to validate the benefits and challenges of implementing DC solutions at scale. This would help build confidence in the technology and provide valuable insights for future development and deployment.
