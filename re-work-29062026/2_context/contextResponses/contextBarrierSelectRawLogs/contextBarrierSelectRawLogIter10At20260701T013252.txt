Based on my expertise, I would select the following five barriers for the expert elicitation exercise:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: The concern revolves around potential power losses, poor quality of electricity, and safety concerns associated with direct current (DC) grids compared to alternating current (AC).
2. Reduced reliability in DC devices: There is a perceived lower level of reliability in DC devices, which may require additional maintenance or replacement over time, increasing costs and reducing overall efficiency.
3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: It can be challenging to identify scenarios where DC solutions offer clear advantages over traditional AC systems, making it difficult for stakeholders to justify the investment in DC infrastructure.
4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): The uncertainty surrounding net metering policies, utility ownership, and the need for agreed-upon standards can create obstacles for the widespread adoption of DC solutions.
5. Lack of pilot projects: A limited number of pilot projects using DC technologies makes it difficult to evaluate their performance in real-world scenarios and gather data that could help address concerns and increase confidence in the technology.
