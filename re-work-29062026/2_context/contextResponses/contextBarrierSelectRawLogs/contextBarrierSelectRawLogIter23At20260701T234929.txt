1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues
2. Reduced reliability in DC devices
3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous
4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards)
5. Lack of pilot projects

These five barriers are significant challenges to the widespread adoption of medium- and low-voltage DC technologies. Power losses, quality, and safety issues can hinder the efficiency and effectiveness of DC systems. Reduced reliability in DC devices can lead to frequent maintenance and downtime, which can be costly and inconvenient for users. A lack of use-cases where DC is advantageous may limit the adoption of these technologies, as potential users may not see a clear benefit to switching from traditional AC systems. Uncertain utility interaction can also pose challenges, as it can create confusion about net metering policies, utility ownership, and agreed standards. Finally, the lack of pilot projects can make it difficult for potential users to see firsthand how DC systems perform in real-world applications, which can be a significant barrier to adoption.
