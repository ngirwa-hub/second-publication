5. lack of pilot projects

These barriers are significant challenges that need to be addressed for the widespread adoption of medium- and low-voltage DC solutions. Power losses, quality, and safety issues are critical concerns in any energy system, and addressing them is essential to ensure the efficiency and safety of DC grids. Reduced reliability in DC devices can deter potential users, as it may lead to frequent failures and maintenance costs. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous highlights the need for more demonstrations and pilot projects to showcase the benefits of DC technologies over traditional AC systems. Uncertain utility interaction is a significant hurdle, as clear guidelines on net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards are necessary to facilitate seamless integration of DC systems into existing energy infrastructures. Lastly, the lack of pilot projects is a barrier that hinders the development and validation of new technologies, making it crucial to invest in more demonstrators to validate the potential benefits and challenges of DC solutions.
