--- generalist_mistral ---
Based on my role as a general expert in renewable energy and DC technologies, I would select the following five barriers:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: The concern about power losses, quality, and safety issues is a significant barrier to the widespread adoption of DC solutions due to potential risks associated with voltage levels and system design.

2. Reduced reliability in DC devices: The perceived reduced reliability of DC devices compared to AC devices can deter investors and end-users from adopting DC technologies, as reliability is a crucial factor in ensuring the stability of power grids.

3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: There is a need for more demonstrable advantages of using DC solutions over traditional AC systems to convince stakeholders and investors of their benefits, especially when considering the costs associated with transitioning to new technologies.

4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): The lack of clarity in terms of utility regulations, net metering policies, and agreed standards for DC systems can create uncertainty and hesitation among potential adopters, as these factors greatly impact the financial viability of DC projects.

5. Lack of pilot projects: Limited experience with pilot projects and real-world implementations of medium- and low-voltage DC technologies makes it difficult to assess their performance and reliability, which can discourage investors and end-users from adopting these solutions.
