Based on my expert role, I would select the following five barriers for consideration:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: The concern about power loss and safety is a significant hurdle for widespread adoption of direct current (DC) technologies. Ensuring that DC systems are reliable, safe, and efficient in terms of energy loss is crucial to their successful implementation.

2. Reduced reliability in DC devices: Although DC technology has several advantages, one challenge lies in maintaining the reliability of DC devices. Developing dependable components and addressing potential weaknesses will be essential for improving the overall performance of DC systems.

3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: To encourage widespread adoption, it's important to demonstrate where DC technologies offer clear benefits over traditional alternating current (AC) solutions. Identifying practical applications and showcasing their advantages will help drive interest and investment in DC technologies.

4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): The relationship between utilities and DC systems is an essential factor affecting the adoption of these technologies. Establishing clear guidelines for net metering, utility ownership, and standardization will help reduce uncertainty and pave the way for more widespread use of DC solutions.

5. Lack of pilot projects: Implementing pilot projects can serve as valuable examples of successful DC applications, helping to build confidence in the technology and attract further investment. Developing and showcasing pilot projects is crucial for demonstrating the potential benefits of DC technologies and driving their adoption.
