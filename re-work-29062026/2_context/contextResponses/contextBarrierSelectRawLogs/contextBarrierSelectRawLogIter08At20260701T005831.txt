Based on my expertise, I would select the following five barriers for the expert elicitation exercise:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues (Barrier 1) - The potential for increased power losses in DC systems compared to AC systems can lead to higher operational costs and reduced efficiency. Ensuring high-quality power, maintaining system safety, and minimizing losses are crucial concerns when implementing DC technologies.

2. Reduced reliability in DC devices (Barrier 3) - The reliability of DC components is often lower than that of AC components due to factors such as higher voltage levels, limited fault tolerance, and lack of redundancy. Improving the reliability of DC devices is essential for widespread adoption.

3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous (Barrier 9) - Demonstrating clear benefits and competitive advantages of DC systems over AC systems is crucial for driving wider acceptance and deployment. Identifying and promoting successful case studies can help to overcome this barrier.

4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards) (Barrier 4) - Establishing clear policies and guidelines for net metering, utility ownership, and standardization is essential for the seamless integration of DC systems with existing grid infrastructure. Addressing these uncertainties can help to accelerate the adoption of DC technologies.

5. Lack of pilot projects (Barrier 5) - Real-world demonstrations and pilot projects showcasing the benefits, challenges, and feasibility of DC systems are vital for building confidence in the technology among stakeholders. Developing and sharing successful pilot project results can help to overcome this barrier.
