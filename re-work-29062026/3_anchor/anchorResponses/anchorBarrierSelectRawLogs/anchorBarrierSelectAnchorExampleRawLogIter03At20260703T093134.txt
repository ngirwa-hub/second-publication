Based on my expertise, I would select the following five barriers for medium- and low-voltage DC technologies:

1. power losses, quality, and safety issues (#1) - The concern over energy losses in DC systems compared to AC systems is a significant barrier, as well as ensuring the quality and safety of DC equipment and systems.
2. reduced reliability in DC devices (#2) - Reliability is crucial for any power system, and there may be concerns about the robustness and longevity of DC components when compared to AC alternatives.
3. lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous (#3) - Demonstrating clear advantages of DC systems over AC systems in specific applications can help drive adoption, but a lack of such examples may slow progress.
4. uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards) (#4) - Clear guidelines for integrating DC systems into the existing grid, particularly regarding net metering, utility involvement, and standardization, are necessary to promote widespread adoption.
5. lack of pilot projects (#9) - Real-world demonstrations of successful DC system implementations can help build confidence in the technology and provide valuable insights for future projects.
