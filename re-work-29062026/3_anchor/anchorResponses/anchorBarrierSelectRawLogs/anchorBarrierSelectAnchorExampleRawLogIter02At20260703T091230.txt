--- mistral-generalist ---
1. power losses, quality and safety issues
2. reduced reliability in DC devices
3. lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous
4. uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards)
5. lack of pilot projects

These five barriers are significant challenges that need to be addressed for the widespread adoption of medium- and low-voltage DC technologies. Power losses, quality, and safety issues are crucial concerns as they directly impact efficiency and cost-effectiveness. Reduced reliability in DC devices is another barrier, as it can lead to system failures and increased maintenance costs. The lack of use-cases where DC is advantageous highlights the need for demonstrators and pilot projects to showcase the benefits of DC technology. Uncertain utility interaction also poses a challenge, as clear standards and agreements are necessary for successful integration with existing power grids. Lastly, the absence of enough pilot projects can hinder the adoption and growth of DC technologies.
