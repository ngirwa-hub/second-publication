Based on the given context, here are five barriers that I would select for discussion:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: These concerns arise due to the direct current's inherent properties, which may lead to higher voltage levels and potential safety hazards. Addressing these challenges is crucial for widespread adoption.
2. Reduced reliability in DC devices: Direct current systems often involve new components and technologies that may not be as well-established or reliable as their alternating current counterparts. Ensuring the reliability of DC devices is essential for end-user confidence and acceptance.
3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: Demonstrating clear benefits over traditional AC systems is necessary to encourage investment in DC technologies. Showcasing successful applications can help build momentum and support for DC solutions.
4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): Establishing clear guidelines and standards for net metering, utility ownership, and interoperability between AC and DC systems is essential to ensure a smooth transition and minimize disruption to existing infrastructure.
5. Lack of pilot projects: Pilot projects are critical for testing and validating the performance of DC technologies in real-world scenarios. Successful demonstrations can help build confidence among stakeholders, leading to increased investment and adoption.
