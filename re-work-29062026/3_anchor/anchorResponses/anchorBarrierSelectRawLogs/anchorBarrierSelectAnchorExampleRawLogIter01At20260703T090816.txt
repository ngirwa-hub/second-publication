--- mistral-generalist ---
Based on my expertise, the following five barriers are selected from the provided list:

1. power losses, quality and safety issues
2. reduced reliability in DC devices
3. lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous
4. uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards)
5. high costs of DC solutions

These barriers are significant challenges that need to be addressed for the widespread adoption of medium- and low-voltage DC technologies. Power losses, quality, and safety issues can lead to inefficiencies and potential risks. Reduced reliability in DC devices may affect the overall performance and longevity of the systems. The lack of use-cases where DC is advantageous limits the market for these technologies, making it difficult to justify the costs associated with their development and implementation. Uncertain utility interaction can create challenges in terms of net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards, potentially hindering the integration of DC solutions into existing power grids. Lastly, high costs of DC solutions are a significant barrier that needs to be addressed for widespread adoption, as they may make these technologies economically unattractive compared to traditional AC-based systems.
