--- mistral-generalist ---
Based on my expertise, I would select the following five barriers for medium- and low-voltage DC technologies:

1. power losses, quality, and safety issues
2. reduced reliability in DC devices
3. uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards)
4. lack of pilot projects
5. high costs of DC solutions

These barriers are significant challenges that need to be addressed for the successful deployment and widespread adoption of medium- and low-voltage DC technologies. Power losses, quality, and safety issues are critical concerns due to the direct current's unique properties compared to alternating current. Reduced reliability in DC devices can lead to system failures and increased maintenance costs. Uncertain utility interaction is a concern as it affects net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards, which are essential for seamless integration with existing grid infrastructure. Lack of pilot projects hinders the development and validation of new technologies, making it difficult for stakeholders to assess their performance and potential benefits. Lastly, high costs of DC solutions can deter potential users and slow down adoption rates.
