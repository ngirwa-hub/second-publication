Based on my expert role, I would select the following five barriers for medium- and low-voltage DC technologies:

1. power losses, quality, and safety issues (Barrier 1) - The efficiency of DC systems can be significantly lower compared to AC systems due to power losses in conversion processes. Ensuring proper insulation, voltage levels, and system design is crucial for maintaining quality and safety.

2. reduced reliability in DC devices (Barrier 2) - While AC systems have been extensively tested and proven reliable over time, the use of DC devices in large-scale applications remains relatively new. Reliability concerns may hinder widespread adoption until more data on their long-term performance is available.

3. lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous (Barrier 3) - Demonstrating clear advantages for adopting DC technologies over AC systems is essential. To encourage wider implementation, it's important to identify and showcase industries or applications where DC systems offer tangible benefits such as reduced energy losses, improved efficiency, or increased flexibility.

4. uncertain utility interaction (Barrier 4) - Clear guidelines, regulations, and standards for grid integration, net metering, utility ownership, and system interoperability are necessary to ensure smooth implementation of DC technologies. Uncertainty in these areas can create barriers for widespread adoption.

5. lack of pilot projects (Barrier 9) - Real-world demonstrations and pilot projects help validate the performance of new technologies, build confidence in their reliability, and provide valuable data for future system design and optimization. A limited number of pilot projects may slow down the adoption rate of DC solutions.
