---

In the context of widespread DC adoption, especially in medium- and low-voltage systems, DC connectors play a vital role. They facilitate the seamless integration of renewable energy sources such as solar panels and storage systems into existing infrastructure. The development of both passive and smart connectors addresses different needs: passive connectors enhance physical robustness necessary for environments with mechanical stresses, while smart connectors integrate advanced electronic capabilities to manage higher voltages and currents effectively.

Furthermore, their application in diverse settings like buildings and ports underscores their importance. In buildings, they enable the integration of distributed photovoltaic systems into DC grids, enhancing energy efficiency and sustainability. Ports, being complex environments with varied power demands, benefit significantly from reliable DC connectors that support onshore power supply, reducing reliance on fossil fuels.

The dual focus on robustness and smart functionality in connector design ensures compatibility with current standards and future scalability of DC technologies. This adaptability is essential for overcoming one of the main barriers to DC adoption: interoperability with existing systems and new installations. Therefore, their development is a key enabler for the broader implementation of DC solutions across different sectors.
