---

The context provided highlights the importance of deploying workable low-voltage (LV) and medium-voltage (MV) direct current (DC) microgrids across various sectors within Europe. Smart and sustainable DC cables are critical to this effort due to several key factors:

1. **Durability and Longevity**: DC systems have different operational characteristics compared to alternating current (AC) systems, particularly concerning the continuous nature of their electrical flow. This necessitates cables that can withstand these conditions without degradation over time. The emphasis on durable materials ensures that these cables maintain performance throughout their lifespan.

2. **Environmental Compatibility**: Given the shift towards sustainability in energy solutions, incorporating environmentally friendly materials into cable design aligns with broader regulatory and societal goals. It reduces environmental impact during both production and disposal phases.

3. **Ease of Adoption**: By designing DC cables to mimic the handling characteristics of standard AC cables, there is a reduced barrier to entry for their adoption. This ergonomic consideration facilitates smoother transitions in existing infrastructures, making it easier for stakeholders across different sectors to integrate these new technologies without extensive retraining or significant changes to current practices.

4. **Versatility Across Sectors**: The ability to use the same cable handling techniques as AC systems allows for broader application across various demonstration sites, including data centers, residential buildings, industrial setups, and ports. This versatility is crucial for demonstrating the practicality and benefits of DC microgrids in diverse environments.

In summary, smart and sustainable DC cables are rated as "Very important" because they address key challenges in transitioning to DC systems, ensuring that these systems can be deployed effectively and sustainably across multiple sectors within Europe.
