These barriers present challenges in the widespread adoption of direct current (DC) technologies. Uncertain utility interactions can create obstacles for net metering and utility ownership, while agreed standards are essential for seamless integration. Lack of use-cases demonstrating the advantage of DC over traditional AC systems can hinder its adoption. Misconceptions and lack of knowledge can lead to lengthy and expensive design and permit processes, adding to the overall cost of DC solutions. High costs are a significant barrier, as they may deter potential users. The absence of pilot projects limits opportunities for demonstrating the benefits of DC technologies and building confidence in their efficiency and reliability. An uncertain regulatory roadmap can create uncertainty for investors and project developers, further hindering the transition to DC technologies.
