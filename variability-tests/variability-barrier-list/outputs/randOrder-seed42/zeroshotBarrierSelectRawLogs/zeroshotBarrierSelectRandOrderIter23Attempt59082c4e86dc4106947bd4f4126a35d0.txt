These barriers pose significant challenges to the widespread adoption of direct current (DC) technologies. Uncertain utility interaction can create obstacles in implementing net metering, utility ownership, and standardization, which are crucial for the efficient operation of DC systems. The lack of trained personnel in DC systems may hinder the deployment and maintenance of these technologies. Misconceptions and lack of knowledge can lead to lengthy and costly design and permit processes, which can discourage potential adopters. Incompatibility of DC systems components can also hinder the scalability and efficiency of these technologies. Lastly, high costs of DC solutions remain a major barrier to their widespread adoption, especially in the context of competing with traditional energy systems.

- uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards)
- lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems
- incompatibility of DC systems components
- high costs of DC solutions
- uncertain regulatory roadmap

These barriers have been selected as they present challenges that need to be addressed in order to accelerate the transition to a sustainable energy system using direct current (DC) technologies. Uncertain utility interaction, including net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards, can create obstacles for the implementation of DC technologies. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems may hinder the widespread adoption of these technologies due to a shortage of skilled workers. Incompatibility of DC systems components can lead to increased costs and complexity in the development and deployment of DC solutions. High costs of DC solutions and an uncertain regulatory roadmap can create financial and policy barriers for the adoption of DC technologies. Public perception and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects, as well as a lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous, can also present challenges in promoting the adoption of these technologies.
