--- mistral-generalist ---
- incompatibility of DC systems components
- high costs of DC solutions
- uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards)
- power losses, quality and safety issues
- lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems

These barriers pose significant challenges in the transition to a more sustainable energy system using direct current (DC) technologies. The incompatibility of DC systems components can hinder the seamless integration and operation of various DC devices. High costs of DC solutions can deter potential adopters due to the initial investment required. Uncertain utility interaction can create obstacles in the implementation of DC technologies, as clear standards and agreements are necessary for net metering, utility ownership, and other aspects. Power losses, quality, and safety issues are concerns that must be addressed to ensure the efficient and safe operation of DC systems. Lastly, a lack of trained personnel in DC systems can slow down the rate of adoption and implementation, as skilled workers are crucial for the design, installation, and maintenance of these systems.
