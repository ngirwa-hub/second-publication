These barriers are significant in the transition to DC technologies. Misconceptions and lack of knowledge can lead to expensive and lengthy design and permit processes, which can deter potential adopters. High costs of DC solutions can make them less competitive compared to traditional AC systems. Uncertain regulatory roadmaps can create uncertainty for investors and project developers. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous can also hinder the adoption of DC technologies. Lastly, uncertain utility interaction, such as net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards, can create challenges for integrating DC systems into the existing power grid.
