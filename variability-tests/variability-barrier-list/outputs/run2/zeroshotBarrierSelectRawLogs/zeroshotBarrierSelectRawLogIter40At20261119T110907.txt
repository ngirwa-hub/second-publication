Based on the provided context and my expert role, the five barriers I would select from the list are:

1. Lack of pilot projects: The implementation of DC technologies can be risky for investors and stakeholders without concrete evidence of their success. Pilot projects help demonstrate the feasibility, benefits, and potential challenges of DC technologies, which can encourage further investment and adoption.

2. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: Public acceptance and support are essential for the successful implementation of new technologies. If the public perceives DC technologies as complex, expensive, or risky, they may be less likely to support or advocate for their use.

3. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Components from different manufacturers may not be compatible, making it difficult to integrate various DC systems. Standardization and interoperability are crucial for the efficient and cost-effective deployment of DC technologies.

4. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Misunderstandings about DC technologies can result in complicated and costly design and permit processes. Increasing public and professional knowledge about DC technologies can help streamline these processes and reduce costs.

5. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The deployment of DC technologies requires skilled professionals who are familiar with their design, installation, and maintenance. A shortage of trained personnel can hinder the widespread adoption of these technologies.

These five barriers represent significant challenges that need to be addressed to accelerate the transition to a sustainable energy system using DC technologies.
