Based on the provided context and my role as a general expert in renewable energy and DC technologies, I would select the following five barriers from the list:

1. Lack of pilot projects: The implementation of DC technologies requires practical demonstrations to prove their efficiency and feasibility. The absence of pilot projects can hinder the adoption and development of DC solutions.

2. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: A positive public perception is crucial for the widespread adoption of new technologies. If people are not ready to support DC projects, it can slow down the transition to a sustainable energy system.

3. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Compatibility issues between different DC system components can cause challenges during the installation and operation of DC systems.

4. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Misunderstandings and insufficient knowledge about DC technologies can lead to lengthy and expensive design and permit processes, which can increase costs and slow down the implementation of DC solutions.

5. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The successful implementation and operation of DC technologies require skilled personnel. A shortage of trained professionals in DC systems can pose a barrier to the widespread adoption of these technologies.

These five barriers are considered significant obstacles to the accelerated transition to a sustainable energy system using DC technologies, as outlined by the Shift2DC project.
