Based on the provided context and my role as a renewable energy and DC technology expert, I would select the following five barriers:

1. Lack of pilot projects: The absence of successful pilot projects can hinder the adoption of DC technologies. Demonstrating the viability and benefits of DC solutions in real-world applications can help build confidence and accelerate the transition.

2. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: Public understanding and acceptance of DC technologies is crucial for their widespread adoption. Building awareness and educating the public about the advantages of DC systems can help drive support and encourage the installation of DC projects.

3. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Ensuring that DC components are compatible and can work seamlessly together is a significant challenge. Standardizing DC components and improving interoperability can help address this issue and facilitate the growth of DC networks.

4. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Misunderstanding of DC technologies can result in overly complex or expensive design and permit processes. Simplifying the design process, providing clear guidelines, and educating stakeholders on DC technologies can help streamline the process and reduce costs.

5. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: As DC technologies become more prevalent, there is a growing need for trained professionals who can design, install, and maintain DC systems. Investing in training programs and education can help address this shortage and ensure the successful deployment of DC solutions.

These five barriers are important to address in order to accelerate the transition to a sustainable energy system using DC technologies.
