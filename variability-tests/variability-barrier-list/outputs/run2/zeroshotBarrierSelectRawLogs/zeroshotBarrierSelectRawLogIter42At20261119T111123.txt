Based on the provided context and my role as an expert in renewable energy and DC technologies, the following five barriers have been selected for the Shift2DC project:

1. Lack of pilot projects: A limited number of pilot projects in the field of DC technologies can hinder the progress and adoption of these solutions, making it difficult to demonstrate their benefits and address potential challenges in real-world scenarios.
2. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: A lack of understanding and acceptance among the public regarding the advantages and benefits of DC systems can create resistance to their deployment, making it challenging to secure support for DC projects.
3. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Compatibility issues between various components of DC systems can lead to integration problems, increased costs, and reduced efficiency, making it difficult to implement and scale up DC technologies.
4. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Inaccurate or incomplete understanding of DC technologies among designers, engineers, and regulatory bodies can lead to extended design and permit processes, adding to the costs and complexity of DC projects.
5. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The limited availability of skilled professionals knowledgeable in DC technologies can create bottlenecks in the development, deployment, and maintenance of DC systems, hindering the widespread adoption of these solutions.

The remaining barriers, while important, were not selected for this exercise:

6. Uncertain regulatory roadmap
7. High costs of DC solutions
8. Power losses, quality, and safety issues
9. Reduced reliability in DC devices
10. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous
11. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards)
