Based on the provided context and my role as an expert in renewable energy and DC technologies, I would select the following five barriers for the Shift2DC project:

1. Lack of pilot projects: The absence of existing pilot projects can hinder the progress and widespread adoption of DC technologies. Pilot projects serve as demonstrations that can help build confidence, showcase benefits, and address technical challenges.

2. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: A positive public perception is crucial for the successful implementation of DC technologies. If the public is not well-informed or does not fully understand the benefits of DC, they may be less likely to support or champion the installation of DC projects.

3. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The compatibility of various DC systems components is essential for seamless integration and efficient operation. Incompatibilities can lead to increased costs, technical issues, and potential safety risks.

4. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Misunderstandings or lack of knowledge about DC technologies can result in lengthy and costly design and permit processes, which can slow down the adoption of DC technologies.

5. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The development and implementation of DC technologies require specialized knowledge and expertise. A shortage of trained personnel can create challenges in designing, installing, and maintaining DC systems, potentially delaying the widespread adoption of these technologies.

These barriers were chosen as they are significant challenges that need to be addressed to accelerate the transition to a sustainable energy system using DC technologies, as outlined by the Shift2DC project.
