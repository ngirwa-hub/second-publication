Based on my expertise and the focus of the Shift2DC project, I would select the following five barriers for consideration:

1. Lack of pilot projects: The absence of demonstrable successful pilot projects can hinder the adoption of DC technologies, as they serve as proof-of-concept and showcase the benefits of transitioning to DC systems.

2. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: The general public's understanding and acceptance of DC technologies plays a crucial role in their widespread adoption. A lack of awareness or skepticism can impede the growth of DC projects.

3. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The interoperability and compatibility issues among different DC systems components can create challenges in the design, implementation, and maintenance of DC projects, leading to higher costs and potential technical difficulties.

4. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: A lack of understanding about DC technologies can result in complex and costly design and permit processes, as regulations may not be well-suited to accommodate DC systems, leading to delays and increased costs.

5. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The availability of skilled workers with expertise in DC technologies is essential for the successful deployment and maintenance of DC projects. A shortage of trained personnel can create bottlenecks in the implementation process.

These five barriers are significant obstacles that need to be addressed to accelerate the transition to a sustainable energy system with DC technologies.
