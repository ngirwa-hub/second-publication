Based on the provided context and my role as a subject-matter expert in DC technologies, the following five barriers from the list are selected:

1. Lack of pilot projects: Insufficient demonstration of DC technologies in real-world scenarios can hinder their adoption and create uncertainty among stakeholders.

2. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: A lack of understanding and acceptance of DC technologies among the public can slow down the adoption process, as well as create resistance to the installation of new DC projects.

3. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The lack of standardization and interoperability among DC system components can increase complexity, costs, and time for project implementation.

4. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Inadequate understanding of DC technologies can result in longer and more expensive design and permit processes, increasing the overall cost and complexity of DC projects.

5. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The shortage of skilled professionals familiar with DC technologies can create challenges in the design, installation, and maintenance of DC systems, potentially leading to increased costs and delays in project implementation.
