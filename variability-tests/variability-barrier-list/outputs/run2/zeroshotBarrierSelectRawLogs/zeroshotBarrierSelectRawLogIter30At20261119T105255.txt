Based on my expertise, the five barriers that I would select from the list are:

1. Lack of pilot projects: The absence of pilot projects can hinder the development and demonstration of DC technologies, making it difficult to evaluate their effectiveness and potential for large-scale implementation.

2. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: Public perception plays a crucial role in the adoption of new technologies. If the public is not ready to support or 'champion' DC projects, it could slow down the transition to a DC-based energy system.

3. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Incompatibility among DC system components can lead to technical challenges and increased costs, hindering the widespread adoption of DC technologies.

4. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Misconceptions and lack of knowledge about DC technologies can lead to complex and costly design and permit processes, potentially discouraging their adoption.

5. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: A shortage of trained personnel can create a bottleneck in the deployment of DC technologies, as there may not be enough professionals available to install, maintain, and repair these systems.
