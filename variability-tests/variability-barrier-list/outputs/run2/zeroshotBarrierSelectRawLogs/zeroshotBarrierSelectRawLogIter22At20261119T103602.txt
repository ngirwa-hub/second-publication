Based on the provided context and my role as a subject-matter expert in DC technologies, the following are the five barriers I would select from the list:

1. Lack of pilot projects: The absence of pilot projects can hinder the widespread adoption of DC technologies. Demonstrating the effectiveness and feasibility of DC solutions through successful pilot projects can attract more investments and encourage broader implementation.

2. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: Public perception plays a significant role in the acceptance and adoption of new technologies. If the public is not adequately informed about the benefits of DC technologies and their role in a sustainable energy system, it may slow down the transition to DC.

3. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Ensuring compatibility between different DC systems components is essential for seamless integration and efficient operation. Incompatibility can lead to technical issues, increased costs, and longer project timelines.

4. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: As DC technologies become more prevalent, there is a growing need for trained professionals who can design, install, and maintain these systems. A lack of trained personnel can pose challenges in terms of project delivery, system performance, and safety.

5. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: A clear and consistent regulatory framework is crucial for the successful deployment of DC technologies. An uncertain regulatory environment can cause delays, increase costs, and create uncertainty for investors and project developers.

These five barriers are significant challenges that need to be addressed to accelerate the transition to a sustainable energy system using DC technologies. The Shift2DC project can help overcome these barriers by promoting the development and demonstration of DC solutions in key sectors and fostering collaboration between stakeholders.
