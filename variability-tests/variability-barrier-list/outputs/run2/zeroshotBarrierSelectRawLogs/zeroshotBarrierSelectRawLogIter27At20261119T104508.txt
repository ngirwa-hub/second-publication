3. Incompatibility of DC systems components
4. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process
5. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems

These five barriers, as selected, present significant challenges to the widespread adoption of direct current (DC) technologies in various sectors, as outlined by the Shift2DC project. Addressing these issues is crucial for the successful transition to a more sustainable energy system.

1. Lack of pilot projects: The absence of pilot projects makes it difficult to demonstrate the benefits and feasibility of DC solutions in real-world applications, which can hinder their adoption and acceptance by stakeholders.
2. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: A lack of understanding and willingness among the public to support DC projects can create obstacles for their implementation and widespread adoption.
3. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Compatibility issues among various DC system components can lead to complexities in system design and integration, which can increase costs and decrease efficiency.
4. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Misconceptions and a lack of knowledge about DC technologies can lead to lengthy and expensive design and permit processes, which can discourage their adoption.
5. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: A shortage of skilled personnel who are familiar with DC technologies can create challenges in the design, installation, and maintenance of DC systems, which can impact their overall performance and reliability.

By addressing these five barriers, the Shift2DC project and other initiatives can help accelerate the transition to a more sustainable energy system through the promotion and widespread adoption of direct current (DC) technologies.
