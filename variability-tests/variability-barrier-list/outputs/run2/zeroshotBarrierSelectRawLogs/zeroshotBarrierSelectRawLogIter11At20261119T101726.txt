Based on my expertise, the five barriers I would select for the Shift2DC project are:

1. Lack of pilot projects: The absence of successful pilot projects can hinder the adoption of DC technologies, as potential investors and stakeholders may be reluctant to commit without evidence of its viability.

2. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: Public acceptance is essential for the widespread adoption of any new technology. If the public perceives DC technologies as complex, costly, or risky, it may slow down the transition to a DC-based energy system.

3. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Interoperability issues between DC components can lead to higher costs, longer installation times, and reduced efficiency, making it challenging for DC technologies to compete with established AC systems.

4. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Insufficient understanding of DC technologies among designers, regulators, and permitting authorities can result in unnecessary complications and delays during the design and approval phases of DC projects, increasing costs and hindering their deployment.

5. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The lack of skilled professionals with expertise in DC technologies can create a bottleneck in the development, installation, and maintenance of DC systems, potentially slowing down the transition to a DC-based energy system.
