--- mistral_generalist ---
Based on the provided context and my role as a general expert in renewable energy and DC technologies, the following are the five barriers I would select from the list:

1. Lack of pilot projects: The absence of pilot projects makes it challenging to demonstrate the practicality and efficiency of DC technologies, hindering their widespread adoption.

2. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: Public understanding and support are crucial for the successful implementation of new energy technologies. A lack of awareness or misconceptions about DC technologies can hinder their acceptance and adoption.

3. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Interoperability issues can arise when different DC systems components are not compatible, making it difficult to create seamless, efficient, and cost-effective DC networks.

4. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Inadequate understanding of DC technologies can lead to unnecessary complications during the design and permit process, resulting in longer project timelines and increased costs.

5. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: Demonstrating the advantages of DC technologies in specific applications is essential for attracting investment and promoting their adoption. A lack of clear use-cases where DC offers significant benefits can hinder their widespread implementation.

These barriers are significant challenges that need to be addressed to accelerate the transition to a sustainable energy system using DC technologies.
