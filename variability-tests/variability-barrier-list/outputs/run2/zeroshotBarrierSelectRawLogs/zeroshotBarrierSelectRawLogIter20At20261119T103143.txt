Based on the provided context and my expert role, I would select the following five barriers to the adoption of DC technologies from the list:

1. Lack of pilot projects: Insufficient demonstration of DC technologies in real-world settings hinders the development of trust and confidence in the technology, making it difficult to attract investment and promote its widespread adoption.

2. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: A lack of understanding and awareness among the public about the benefits and advantages of DC technologies can lead to resistance or reluctance to adopt them, making it challenging to drive the transition to a DC-based energy system.

3. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The lack of standardization and interoperability among DC components can make it difficult to design and implement systems that are reliable, efficient, and cost-effective, which can deter potential adopters.

4. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Incorrect assumptions or insufficient knowledge about DC technologies can result in complicated and time-consuming design and permit processes, increasing costs and potentially deterring potential adopters.

5. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: A shortage of skilled professionals with expertise in DC technologies can make it challenging to design, install, and maintain DC systems, which can lead to higher costs, reliability issues, and potential safety risks.

These barriers highlight the need for focused efforts to address the challenges facing the widespread adoption of DC technologies, as part of the Shift2DC project's goal of promoting the use of DC technologies in various sectors.
