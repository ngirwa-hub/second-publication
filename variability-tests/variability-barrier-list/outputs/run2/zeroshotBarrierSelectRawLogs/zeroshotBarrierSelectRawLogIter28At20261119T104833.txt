Based on the provided context and my role as a subject-matter expert in DC technologies, I would select the following five barriers for the Shift2DC project:

1. Lack of pilot projects: Insufficient demonstration and implementation of DC technologies in real-world settings can hinder the adoption and validation of their benefits and potential.

2. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: A general lack of awareness and understanding among the public about the advantages of DC technologies may result in reluctance to support and invest in DC projects.

3. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The absence of industry standards and compatibility issues among various DC system components can lead to higher costs, complexity, and reduced efficiency in DC power systems.

4. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Limited understanding of DC technologies among designers, regulators, and other stakeholders can result in lengthy and costly design and permit processes, potentially slowing down the adoption of DC technologies.

5. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: A shortage of skilled professionals familiar with DC technologies can lead to installation and maintenance challenges, ultimately impacting the reliability and efficiency of DC power systems.

These five barriers were chosen based on their significant impact on the widespread adoption and integration of DC technologies in various sectors, as outlined in the Shift2DC project context.
