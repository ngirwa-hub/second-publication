Based on the context of the Shift2DC project and my role as a subject-matter expert in DC technologies, the following five barriers I would select are:

1. Lack of pilot projects: The absence of well-documented and successful pilot projects can make it difficult for investors and stakeholders to evaluate the potential benefits and risks associated with DC technologies.

2. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations: The general public's understanding and acceptance of DC technologies are critical for their widespread adoption. If the public perceives DC technologies as complex, expensive, or less efficient compared to AC technologies, it could slow down the transition to DC systems.

3. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Interoperability issues between different DC components can lead to compatibility problems, making it challenging to design and implement large-scale DC systems.

4. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Misunderstandings and misinformation about DC technologies can result in lengthy and costly design and permit processes, as well as increased risks during construction and operation.

5. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The limited availability of skilled professionals familiar with DC technologies can pose challenges in the deployment, maintenance, and operation of DC systems, potentially leading to increased costs and safety concerns.

By addressing these barriers, the Shift2DC project can help accelerate the transition to a more sustainable energy system using DC technologies.
