Based on the context and my role as a subject-matter expert in renewable energy and DC technologies, I select the following five barriers for the Shift2DC project:

1. Lack of pilot projects: The lack of successful pilot projects can hinder the adoption of DC technologies, as they provide valuable evidence of their feasibility, benefits, and potential challenges.

2. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: Public acceptance and support are crucial for the widespread adoption of new technologies. A lack of awareness or understanding of DC technologies might lead to resistance from the public, which can delay their implementation.

3. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Compatibility issues among different DC systems components can lead to integration problems, increased costs, and reduced efficiency. Standardizing DC components and interfaces is essential for ensuring seamless integration.

4. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Misunderstandings about the technical requirements of DC systems can result in lengthy and expensive design and permit processes, making DC solutions less competitive compared to AC alternatives.

5. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The scarcity of skilled professionals familiar with DC technologies can lead to difficulties in the design, installation, and maintenance of DC systems. Training and upskilling programs are necessary to address this issue.

These five barriers, if addressed effectively, can help accelerate the transition to a sustainable energy system using DC technologies.
