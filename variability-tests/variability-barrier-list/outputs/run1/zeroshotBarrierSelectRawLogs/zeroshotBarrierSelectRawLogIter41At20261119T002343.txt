--- mistral_generalist ---
Based on the provided context and my role as a renewable energy and DC technology expert, the following five barriers from the list are most relevant to the Shift2DC project:

1. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: This barrier is significant because the project aims to promote the use of DC technologies, but there may not be enough compelling reasons or applications that demonstrate the benefits of DC over AC in certain sectors (ports, industry, data centers, and buildings).

2. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): This barrier is crucial because the success of DC technologies relies on a clear understanding and agreement between DC proponents and utility companies regarding net metering, ownership, and standards.

3. Lack of pilot projects: Pilot projects are essential for demonstrating the feasibility and effectiveness of new technologies, and the absence of such projects could hinder the adoption of DC technologies.

4. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: Public support is crucial for the successful implementation of any renewable energy project, and the public's understanding and acceptance of DC technologies will play a significant role in their widespread adoption.

5. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Compatibility issues can lead to increased costs, reduced efficiency, and potential safety risks, making it a significant barrier to the widespread adoption of DC technologies.
