Based on the context provided and my expertise, I would select the following five barriers from the list:

1. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: This barrier indicates that there is a need for more demonstrations and case studies that show the benefits of DC technologies over traditional AC systems, especially in the sectors targeted by the Shift2DC project.

2. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): This barrier highlights the need for clear guidelines and standards for how DC systems interact with the existing utility grid, including net metering and utility ownership.

3. Lack of pilot projects: Pilot projects are essential for testing and validating new technologies. The absence of enough pilot projects in DC technologies might hinder their widespread adoption.

4. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: Public acceptance and support are crucial for the successful implementation of new technologies. If the public is not yet familiar with DC technologies or does not fully understand their benefits, it could slow down their adoption.

5. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Compatibility issues between different DC components can lead to increased costs and complexities, which might discourage potential adopters from implementing DC technologies.

These barriers were chosen because they directly impact the successful transition to a sustainable energy system using DC technologies, as outlined in the Shift2DC project. Addressing these barriers could help accelerate the adoption of DC technologies in the targeted sectors.
