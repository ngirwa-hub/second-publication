--- mistral_generalist ---
In response to the expert elicitation exercise, I will select the following five barriers to the widespread adoption of Direct Current (DC) technologies as outlined in the Shift2DC project:

1. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: There is a need for more demonstrable benefits and clear-cut advantages of DC technologies over traditional Alternating Current (AC) systems to convince stakeholders and investors of their value.

2. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): Clarification is needed regarding the roles and responsibilities of utilities, net metering policies, and the development of agreed-upon standards for DC systems to ensure seamless integration with the existing grid infrastructure.

3. Lack of pilot projects: The absence of successful DC pilot projects serves as a barrier to gaining real-world evidence of the effectiveness and feasibility of DC technologies. More pilot projects are required to build confidence and validate the benefits of DC systems.

4. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: Educating the public and fostering a positive perception of DC technologies is essential to drive demand for DC systems and encourage grassroots support for their adoption.

5. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The fragmented nature of the DC industry results in a lack of interoperability between various DC components, making it difficult to create cohesive, scalable DC solutions. Greater standardization and collaboration are required to address this issue.

These selected barriers focus on the need for increased understanding, demonstration, and standardization of DC technologies to facilitate their widespread adoption and accelerate the transition to a more sustainable energy system as envisioned by the Shift2DC project.
