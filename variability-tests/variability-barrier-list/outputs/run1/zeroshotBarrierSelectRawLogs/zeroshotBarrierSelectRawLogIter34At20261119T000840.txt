--- mistral_generalist ---
Based on the provided context and my role as an expert in renewable energy and DC technologies, I would select the following five barriers from the list:

1. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous
2. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards)
3. Lack of pilot projects
4. Incompatibility of DC systems components
5. High costs of DC solutions

These barriers are significant challenges that need to be addressed in order to accelerate the transition to a sustainable energy system using DC technologies, as proposed by the Shift2DC project.

1. Lack of use-cases: This barrier highlights the need for demonstrating the advantages of DC technologies in various sectors such as ports, industry, data centers, and buildings.
2. Uncertain utility interaction: This barrier emphasizes the importance of clear policies, standards, and regulations regarding net metering, utility ownership, and other aspects of DC systems interaction with the existing grid.
3. Lack of pilot projects: Pilot projects are essential for testing and validating the performance of DC technologies in real-world conditions.
4. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The lack of standardization and interoperability among different DC system components can hinder the wide-scale adoption of DC technologies.
5. High costs of DC solutions: The cost of DC solutions is often a major obstacle to their widespread implementation, particularly in comparison to traditional AC systems.

By addressing these barriers, the Shift2DC project can help promote the use of DC technologies and contribute to a more sustainable energy system.
