Based on the context and my expertise, the following five barriers from the list are relevant to the transition to DC technologies as per the Shift2DC project:

1. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: This barrier refers to the absence of clear and compelling reasons for switching to DC technologies in various sectors. Identifying and showcasing successful use-cases can help drive the adoption of DC technologies.

2. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): Establishing clear guidelines and standards for net metering, utility ownership, and agreed-upon standards is crucial for the successful integration of DC technologies into the existing power grid.

3. Lack of pilot projects: The development and demonstration of pilot projects are essential for validating the benefits and feasibility of DC technologies in real-world scenarios. This can help build confidence in the technology and encourage wider adoption.

4. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Ensuring compatibility among various DC systems components is essential for seamless integration and operation. Standards and guidelines should be established to promote interoperability and ease the adoption of DC technologies.

5. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: A clear and predictable regulatory environment is vital for the long-term success of DC technologies. Establishing a supportive regulatory framework that addresses issues such as permitting, safety, and environmental impact can help accelerate the transition to DC technologies.

These five barriers are considered the most relevant to the Shift2DC project and the promotion of DC technologies in the sectors of ports, industry, data centers, and buildings.
