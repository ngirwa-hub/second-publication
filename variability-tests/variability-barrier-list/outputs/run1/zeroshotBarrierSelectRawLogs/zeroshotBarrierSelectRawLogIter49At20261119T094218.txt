1. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: This barrier refers to the limited understanding and application of DC technologies in various sectors, which may hinder its widespread adoption.

2. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): This barrier relates to the lack of clear regulations and guidelines for utility companies regarding net metering, ownership, and standardization of DC systems, which can create uncertainty and impede the adoption of DC technologies.

3. Lack of pilot projects: The absence of pilot projects demonstrating the feasibility, benefits, and efficiency of DC technologies can hinder their acceptance and implementation in various sectors.

4. Incompatibility of DC systems components: This barrier refers to the challenges in ensuring compatibility among different DC components, which can lead to integration issues and increased costs.

5. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: This barrier refers to the lack of a clear and consistent regulatory framework for DC technologies, which can create uncertainty for investors and project developers, and potentially slow down the adoption of DC technologies.

These barriers are significant challenges that need to be addressed to accelerate the transition to a sustainable energy system using DC technologies, as outlined by the Shift2DC project.
