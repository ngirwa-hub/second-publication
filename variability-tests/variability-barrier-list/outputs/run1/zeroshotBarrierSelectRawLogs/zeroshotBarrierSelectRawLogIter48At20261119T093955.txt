Based on the provided context and expertise in renewable energy and DC technologies, I have selected five barriers that are most relevant to the transition to a sustainable energy system using direct current (DC) technologies.

1. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous - This barrier can be addressed by promoting the development and demonstration of DC solutions in various sectors, such as ports, industry, data centers, and buildings, to showcase their benefits and applications.

2. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards) - To overcome this barrier, the Shift2DC project can facilitate discussions and agreements between utilities and other stakeholders to ensure proper net metering and other aspects of interaction.

3. Lack of pilot projects - Addressing this barrier can be achieved by promoting and demonstrating the effectiveness of DC solutions through pilot projects, which can provide valuable data and insights for further adoption.

4. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects - Educating the public about the benefits of DC systems and encouraging the adoption of DC technologies can help overcome this barrier.

5. Incompatibility of DC systems components - Ensuring proper interoperability and compatibility of components in DC systems can help address this barrier, which can be achieved through standardization efforts and collaboration among industry stakeholders.
