--- mistral_generalist ---
In the context of the Shift2DC project, the following five barriers for the transition to a sustainable energy system using direct current (DC) technologies are selected:

1. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: There is a need for more demonstration projects and case studies showcasing the benefits of DC technology in various sectors (ports, industry, data centers, and buildings) to promote its adoption.

2. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): A clear understanding and agreement between utilities and DC project developers regarding net metering policies, utility ownership, and standardization are essential for the successful implementation of DC solutions.

3. Lack of pilot projects: The development and demonstration of pilot projects that show the feasibility, efficiency, and cost-effectiveness of DC technology is crucial in encouraging its widespread adoption.

4. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: Overcoming public misconceptions and increasing awareness about the benefits of DC technology is necessary for driving societal acceptance and supporting the installation of DC projects.

5. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Ensuring interoperability and compatibility among different DC system components is essential for the successful deployment and operation of DC solutions.

These barriers, when addressed, can help accelerate the transition to a more sustainable energy system using DC technologies.
