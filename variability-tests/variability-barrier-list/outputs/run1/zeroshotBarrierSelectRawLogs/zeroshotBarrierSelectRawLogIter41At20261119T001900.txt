Based on the provided context and my expertise, I would select the following five barriers to the widespread adoption of DC technologies as part of the Shift2DC project:

1. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: This barrier refers to the limited applications where direct current (DC) technology provides a clear advantage over traditional alternating current (AC) systems. Identifying and demonstrating these use-cases is crucial to drive the adoption of DC technologies.

2. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): This barrier concerns the unclear rules and regulations regarding net metering, utility ownership, and the adoption of agreed standards for DC systems. Clarifying these aspects is essential for the successful integration of DC technologies into the existing energy infrastructure.

3. Lack of pilot projects: Implementing DC technologies at a large scale requires demonstrating their efficiency and reliability through pilot projects. The lack of these projects can hinder the widespread adoption of DC solutions.

4. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: This barrier relates to public awareness and acceptance of DC technologies. Overcoming this challenge requires educating the public about the benefits of DC systems and encouraging their active participation in promoting and adopting these technologies.

5. Incompatibility of DC systems components: This barrier refers to the challenges in integrating different components of DC systems due to a lack of standardization and interoperability issues. Addressing these compatibility problems is essential for the smooth operation and scalability of DC systems.

These five barriers, if addressed effectively, can contribute significantly to the successful implementation of the Shift2DC project and the transition to a more sustainable energy system using direct current technologies.
