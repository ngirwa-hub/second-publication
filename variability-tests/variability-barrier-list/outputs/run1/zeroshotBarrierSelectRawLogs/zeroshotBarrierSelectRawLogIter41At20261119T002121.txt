Based on the provided context and my expert role, I would select the following five barriers for consideration in the Shift2DC project:

1. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: Addressing the need for clear and compelling use-cases that demonstrate the benefits of DC technologies is crucial for driving widespread adoption.

2. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): Establishing clear guidelines and standards for utility interaction, including net metering and utility ownership, will help facilitate the integration of DC technologies within the existing grid infrastructure.

3. Lack of pilot projects: Developing and showcasing successful pilot projects can help demonstrate the feasibility and benefits of DC technologies, fostering confidence and encouraging broader adoption.

4. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: Addressing public perceptions and fostering support for DC projects is essential for overcoming resistance and securing the necessary funding and resources for widespread adoption.

5. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Ensuring compatibility between various DC systems components, such as solar panels, wind turbines, and energy storage systems, is critical for streamlining the integration process and reducing costs associated with incompatibility issues.

These five barriers have been selected as they directly impact the transition to a sustainable energy system using DC technologies and require immediate attention to accelerate the adoption of DC solutions in the specified sectors.
