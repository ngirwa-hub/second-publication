2. Uncertain utility interaction is a concern, as clear guidelines for net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards are essential for the successful integration of DC systems into the existing grid infrastructure.
3. Lack of pilot projects serves as a barrier to proving the feasibility and effectiveness of DC technologies in real-world settings. More pilot projects are needed to validate the advantages of DC systems and pave the way for wider implementation.
4. Incompatibility of DC systems components can hinder the adoption of DC technology, as the availability of compatible components is essential for seamless integration and efficient operation.
5. High costs of DC solutions are a significant barrier to widespread adoption, as the initial investment required for DC systems can be prohibitive for many stakeholders. Addressing this barrier will require cost reductions through advancements in technology, economies of scale, and government incentives.
