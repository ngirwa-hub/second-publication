Based on the given context and my role as an expert in renewable energy and DC technologies, I would select the following five barriers for the Shift2DC project:

1. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: This barrier indicates a need for more demonstration projects and case studies that clearly show the benefits of DC technologies over AC in specific applications, which can help drive adoption.

2. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): Clarity and standardization are crucial for the successful integration of DC technologies into the grid. Addressing uncertainties in net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards will help facilitate the seamless integration of DC systems with the existing grid infrastructure.

3. Lack of pilot projects: The implementation of pilot projects is essential for testing and validating the performance, cost-effectiveness, and scalability of DC technologies. More pilot projects can help demonstrate the advantages of DC solutions and provide valuable insights for further development.

4. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: The general public's understanding and acceptance of DC technologies play a significant role in their adoption. Improving public perception through education and awareness campaigns can help drive the adoption of DC solutions in various sectors.

5. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Ensuring compatibility between various DC systems components is crucial for the efficient and reliable operation of DC networks. Addressing incompatibility issues can help reduce costs, improve efficiency, and increase the reliability of DC systems.

These five barriers are important considerations for the Shift2DC project and should be addressed to accelerate the transition to a sustainable energy system using DC technologies.
