Based on my expertise, I would select the following five barriers to the acceleration of the transition to a sustainable energy system through the promotion of direct current (DC) technologies:

1. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: Understanding and demonstrating the benefits of DC technologies in various sectors is crucial for their widespread adoption.
2. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): Clear guidelines and standards regarding the integration of DC systems with existing utility infrastructure are necessary to ensure smooth operation and efficient energy transfer.
3. Lack of pilot projects: Demonstrating the feasibility and effectiveness of DC solutions through pilot projects is essential to build confidence and encourage further investment in the technology.
4. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: Addressing misconceptions and increasing public awareness about the benefits of DC technologies can help stimulate demand and facilitate their implementation.
5. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Standardizing components and ensuring interoperability among various DC systems is essential to reduce costs, simplify installation, and promote widespread adoption.

These barriers have been selected as they represent significant challenges that need to be addressed to facilitate the successful transition to a DC-based energy system.
