--- mistral_generalist ---
1. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous
2. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards)
3. Lack of pilot projects
4. Incompatibility of DC systems components
5. High costs of DC solutions

These five barriers present significant challenges to the widespread adoption of DC technologies in various sectors, as outlined in the Shift2DC project. Addressing these barriers will be crucial for accelerating the transition to a more sustainable energy system.

1. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: Demonstrating clear advantages of DC technology over AC in specific applications is essential for promoting its adoption.

2. Uncertain utility interaction: Clarifying net metering policies, addressing utility ownership concerns, and establishing agreed-upon standards will help build trust and facilitate the integration of DC technologies into the energy grid.

3. Lack of pilot projects: Developing and showcasing successful pilot projects can help build confidence in DC technologies and demonstrate their potential benefits.

4. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Standardizing components and interfaces will help reduce complexity and costs, making DC solutions more appealing to manufacturers, installers, and end-users.

5. High costs of DC solutions: Reducing the cost of DC technologies, either through technological advancements or economies of scale, will make them more competitive with traditional AC solutions.

By addressing these barriers, the Shift2DC project can help pave the way for a more sustainable energy system that leverages the advantages of DC technologies in key sectors like ports, industry, data centers, and buildings.
