Based on the provided context and my expertise, I would select the following five barriers for the Shift2DC project:

1. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: The absence of clear and compelling use-cases for DC technologies can hinder their adoption, making it challenging to demonstrate their benefits and potential for widespread implementation.

2. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): Uncertainty regarding utility interaction, including net metering policies, utility ownership structures, and agreed-upon standards, can create barriers for the deployment of DC technologies, as it may impact the financial viability and practicality of projects.

3. Lack of pilot projects: The absence of pilot projects can impede the development and demonstration of DC solutions, making it difficult to assess their performance, identify best practices, and build confidence in their potential benefits.

4. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: A lack of public understanding and acceptance of DC technologies can present a challenge, as it may impact the willingness of individuals and organizations to support and invest in DC projects, either as end-users or project champions.

5. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The incompatibility of DC systems components can create challenges for the integration and interoperability of DC technologies, making it difficult to design and implement efficient and cost-effective solutions.

These barriers, if addressed, can help accelerate the transition to a more sustainable energy system through the promotion of direct current technologies.
