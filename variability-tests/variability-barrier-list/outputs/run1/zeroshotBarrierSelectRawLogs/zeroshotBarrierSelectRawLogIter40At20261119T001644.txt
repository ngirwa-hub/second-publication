Based on the provided context and my role as a general expert in renewable energy and DC technologies, the following are the five barriers I would select from the list:

1. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: It's crucial to identify and demonstrate the benefits of DC technologies in various sectors to encourage their adoption.

2. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): Clear guidelines and standards for utility interaction are necessary to ensure smooth integration of DC technologies into the existing grid infrastructure.

3. Lack of pilot projects: Pilot projects are essential to test and validate the performance of DC technologies in real-world scenarios, which can help build confidence and attract investment.

4. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: Public acceptance and support play a significant role in the successful implementation of DC projects. Building awareness and educating the public about the benefits of DC technologies can help foster a more favorable attitude towards their adoption.

5. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Ensuring compatibility between different components of DC systems is essential to ensure smooth operation and efficient energy transfer. Standardization efforts can help address this issue.

These barriers, if addressed, could contribute to a more seamless transition to a sustainable energy system using DC technologies.
