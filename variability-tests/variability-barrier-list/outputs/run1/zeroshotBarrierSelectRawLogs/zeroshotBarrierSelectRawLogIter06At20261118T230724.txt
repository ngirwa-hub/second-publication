--- mistral_generalist ---
1. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous
2. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards)
3. Lack of pilot projects
4. Incompatibility of DC systems components
5. High costs of DC solutions

These five barriers have been selected as they directly impact the adoption and implementation of direct current (DC) technologies in various sectors, such as ports, industry, data centers, and buildings. Addressing these challenges is crucial for the success of the Shift2DC project and the transition to a more sustainable energy system.

1. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: The absence of clear and compelling advantages for using DC over traditional alternating current (AC) technologies can hinder the adoption of DC solutions in various applications.

2. Uncertain utility interaction: Uncertainty regarding net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards can create obstacles for integrating DC systems into the existing grid infrastructure, potentially slowing down the widespread adoption of DC technologies.

3. Lack of pilot projects: The lack of pilot projects showcasing the benefits and feasibility of DC solutions can make it difficult for stakeholders to gain confidence in the technology and invest in its development.

4. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The lack of standardization in DC components can lead to compatibility issues, making it challenging to design and implement efficient and cost-effective DC systems.

5. High costs of DC solutions: The higher initial costs of DC solutions compared to AC technologies can be a significant barrier to their adoption, particularly in applications where cost-effectiveness is a key concern.
