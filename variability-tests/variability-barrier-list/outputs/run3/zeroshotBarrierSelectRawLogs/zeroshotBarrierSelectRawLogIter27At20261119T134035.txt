Based on the expert elicitation exercise and considering the context of the Shift2DC project, I have identified the following five barriers to the adoption of direct current (DC) technologies:

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: DC systems require compatibility of their components to ensure smooth and reliable operation. Incompatibility can lead to system failures, increased maintenance costs, and decreased efficiency.
2. Lack of knowledge and misconceptions: The lack of knowledge and misconceptions about DC systems can lead to lengthy and expensive design and permit processes. This can result in delays, increased costs, and reduced adoption of DC technologies.
3. Lack of trained personnel in DC systems: The absence of trained personnel with knowledge and experience in designing, installing, and maintaining DC systems can hinder the adoption of these technologies. This can lead to suboptimal system performance, increased costs, and safety risks.
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Uncertainty regarding the regulatory framework and standards for DC technologies can create barriers to adoption. This can result in increased costs, delays, and a lack of confidence in the technology.
5. High costs of DC solutions: The high costs of DC technologies can be a significant barrier to adoption, as it may be difficult for businesses and organizations to justify the investment when compared to traditional AC solutions. This can lead to reduced adoption and slower progress towards a sustainable energy system.
