3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The availability of trained personnel is essential for the successful deployment and maintenance of DC systems. A lack of trained personnel can lead to delays, increased costs, and reduced efficiency.
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Unclear or inconsistent regulations can create uncertainty and increase costs for developers and investors in DC technologies.
5. High costs of DC solutions: The high costs of DC solutions compared to AC systems can make it difficult for projects to secure funding and compete with established technologies.

The remaining barriers are also important considerations in the transition to DC technologies, but the five selected barriers are deemed to have a more significant impact on the project's objectives.

--- mistral_subject-matter ---
1. Incompatibility of DC systems components
2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process
3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap
5. High costs of DC solutions

These five barriers were selected as they directly impact the adoption and implementation of DC technologies in various sectors, including ports, industry, data centers, and buildings. Addressing these challenges is essential to accelerate the transition to a sustainable energy system as envisioned by the Shift2DC project.

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The lack of standardization and interoperability among DC components can lead to increased costs, complexity, and delays in the deployment of DC systems.
2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Limited understanding of DC technologies can result in inefficient design processes, which can lead to higher costs and extended permit approval times.
3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: A shortage of skilled workers familiar with DC technologies can hinder the implementation and maintenance of DC systems, potentially leading to technical issues and increased costs.
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Unclear regulations and standards for DC systems can create uncertainty for investors and project developers, which can slow down the adoption of DC technologies.
5. High costs of DC solutions: Higher costs associated with DC technologies compared to AC solutions can deter potential adopters, making it difficult to compete with established AC infrastructure.

While other barriers such as power losses, quality and safety issues, reduced reliability in DC devices, lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous, uncertain utility interaction, and lack of pilot projects are also important, the selected barriers directly impact the feasibility and cost-effectiveness of implementing DC technologies in various sectors.
