Based on the provided context and my role as an expert in renewable energy and DC technologies, the following five barriers from the list seem particularly relevant for the Shift2DC project:

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: This barrier arises due to the lack of standardization in DC components, which can lead to compatibility issues and increased costs during system integration.

2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Inadequate understanding of DC technologies can result in prolonged and costly design and permit procedures, potentially hindering the widespread adoption of DC solutions.

3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: As DC technologies are still emerging, there is a shortage of skilled professionals with expertise in designing, installing, and maintaining DC systems, which can slow down the transition to DC-based energy systems.

4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Regulations and policies related to DC technologies are still developing, creating uncertainty for investors and project developers, which can discourage the deployment of DC solutions.

5. High costs of DC solutions: Compared to traditional AC-based systems, DC solutions can be more expensive due to factors such as higher component costs and the need for conversion equipment. This can make DC technologies less attractive for some applications.

These barriers highlight the challenges faced in promoting the use of DC technologies and emphasize the need for addressing them to accelerate the transition to a sustainable energy system as envisioned by the Shift2DC project.
