--- mistral_subject-matter ---
1. Incompatibility of DC systems components
2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process
3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap
5. High costs of DC solutions

These five barriers have been selected based on their potential impact on the widespread adoption of DC technologies in the sectors mentioned: ports, industry, data centers, and buildings, as part of the Shift2DC project. The chosen barriers directly address technical, regulatory, and societal challenges that need to be overcome to accelerate the transition to a sustainable energy system using DC technologies.

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The diverse nature of DC systems components can lead to compatibility issues, which can increase costs and complexity in system design and deployment.
2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Limited understanding of DC technologies can result in lengthy and costly design and permit processes due to a lack of familiarity with the unique aspects of DC systems.
3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: A shortage of skilled professionals with expertise in DC technologies can hinder the deployment and maintenance of DC systems, leading to increased costs and delays.
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Ambiguity in regulations can create uncertainty for stakeholders, making it difficult to invest in and deploy DC technologies.
5. High costs of DC solutions: The upfront costs of DC solutions can be a barrier to their adoption, especially in comparison to traditional AC systems.

The remaining barriers will not be selected for this exercise, but they are also important considerations in the transition to DC technologies:

6. Power losses, quality and safety issues: Power losses, quality issues, and safety concerns can arise in DC systems, and addressing these challenges is crucial for the widespread adoption of DC technologies.
7. Reduced reliability in DC devices: The reliability of DC devices may be lower than that of AC devices, which can lead to increased maintenance costs and downtime.
8. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: Demonstrating the advantages of DC technologies in specific use-cases is essential to building a strong case for their widespread adoption.
9. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): The interaction between DC technologies and utilities is an important consideration, as it can impact factors such as net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards.
10. Lack of pilot projects: Pilot projects help demonstrate the feasibility and benefits of DC technologies, which can help build momentum for their adoption.
11. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: Public perception plays a significant role in the adoption of new technologies, and addressing any misconceptions or concerns about DC technologies is essential to building support for their deployment.
