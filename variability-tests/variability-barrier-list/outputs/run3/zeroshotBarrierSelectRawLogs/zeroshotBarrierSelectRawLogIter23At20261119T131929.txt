--- mistral_generalist ---
1. Incompatibility of DC systems components
2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process
3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap
5. High costs of DC solutions

These five barriers were selected as they represent significant challenges in the transition to a sustainable energy system using direct current (DC) technologies.

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The use of DC systems requires components that are specifically designed for DC operation. However, many existing components are designed for alternating current (AC) systems, making it difficult to integrate DC systems into existing infrastructure.
2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: There is a lack of understanding about DC systems among designers, engineers, and permitting authorities, leading to long and expensive design and permit processes.
3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: There is a shortage of trained personnel who are familiar with DC systems, making it difficult to design, install, and maintain DC systems.
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Regulations and standards for DC systems are still evolving, making it difficult for developers to navigate the regulatory landscape and secure permits for DC projects.
5. High costs of DC solutions: DC systems can be more expensive to install and maintain than AC systems, making it difficult to justify the investment in DC technology, especially in the early stages of its development.
