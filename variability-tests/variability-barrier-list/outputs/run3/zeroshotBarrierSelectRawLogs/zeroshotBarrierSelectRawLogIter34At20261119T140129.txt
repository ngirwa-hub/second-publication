--- mistral_subject-matter ---
1. Incompatibility of DC systems components
2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process
3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap
5. High costs of DC solutions

These five barriers are the most significant ones in accelerating the transition to a sustainable energy system by promoting the use of direct current (DC) technologies, as outlined in the Shift2DC project.

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The diversity and lack of standardization of DC components pose challenges in seamless integration and interoperability, which can lead to increased costs and complexity in DC power systems.

2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Limited understanding of DC technologies can result in prolonged design processes, extensive permitting, and higher costs due to the need for additional studies and assessments.

3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The scarcity of experts and skilled workers familiar with DC technologies can slow down the development, deployment, and maintenance of DC systems, ultimately hindering their widespread adoption.

4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Ambiguous or inconsistent regulations regarding DC technologies create uncertainties for investors and project developers, making it challenging to plan and execute DC projects.

5. High costs of DC solutions: The initial costs associated with DC technologies can be a significant barrier to their widespread adoption, as they often require more expensive components and infrastructure compared to AC systems.
