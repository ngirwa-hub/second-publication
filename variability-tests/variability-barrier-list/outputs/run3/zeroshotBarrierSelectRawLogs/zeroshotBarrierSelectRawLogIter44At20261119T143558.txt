Based on the context provided for the Shift2DC project, I have selected the following five barriers to the widespread adoption of DC technologies:

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The lack of standardization in DC components can lead to compatibility issues, making it difficult to integrate various components into a cohesive system.

2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Limited understanding of DC technologies can lead to longer and more expensive design and permit processes due to a lack of familiarity with DC systems among designers, regulators, and permitting authorities.

3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The shortage of skilled workers familiar with DC technologies can slow down the deployment and maintenance of DC systems, increasing costs and decreasing efficiency.

4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: The evolving regulatory landscape for DC technologies can create uncertainty and hesitancy among stakeholders, hindering investment and widespread adoption.

5. High costs of DC solutions: The higher initial cost of DC solutions compared to AC alternatives can deter potential adopters, making it challenging to scale up the use of DC technologies.

These barriers can be addressed by promoting education and training programs, developing industry standards, and advocating for supportive regulations and policies to drive down costs and facilitate the growth of DC technologies.
