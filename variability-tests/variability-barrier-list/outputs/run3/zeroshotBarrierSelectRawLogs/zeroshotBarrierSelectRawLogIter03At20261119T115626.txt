--- mistral_generalist ---
1. Incompatibility of DC systems components
2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process
3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap
5. High costs of DC solutions

These five barriers have been selected as they represent significant challenges to the widespread adoption of DC technologies in various sectors, as outlined in the Shift2DC project.

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The fragmentation of the DC market and the lack of standardization can lead to incompatibility issues between different components, making integration difficult and costly.

2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Limited understanding and familiarity with DC technologies among designers, regulators, and permitting authorities can result in lengthy and expensive design and permit processes, potentially hindering the deployment of DC solutions.

3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The relatively small market share of DC technologies compared to AC systems means there is a shortage of skilled professionals who are familiar with DC technologies, making it difficult to design, install, and maintain DC systems.

4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: The uncertainty surrounding the regulatory landscape for DC technologies, including net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards, can create barriers for investors and project developers, making it challenging to plan and implement DC projects.

5. High costs of DC solutions: While the costs of DC technologies are decreasing, they are still generally more expensive than their AC counterparts, making it difficult for many projects to justify the additional investment required for DC systems. Addressing these barriers will be crucial in accelerating the transition to a sustainable energy system through the promotion of DC technologies.
