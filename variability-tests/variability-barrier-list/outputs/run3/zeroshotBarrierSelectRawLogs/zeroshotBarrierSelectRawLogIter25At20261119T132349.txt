3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap
5. High costs of DC solutions

These five barriers have been selected as they represent significant challenges in the transition to DC technologies, as outlined by the Shift2DC project. They encompass technical, regulatory, and economic challenges that need to be addressed to facilitate the widespread adoption of DC power systems.

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The current proliferation of AC-based systems presents a challenge for the integration of DC components, as compatibility issues may arise, hindering the widespread adoption of DC technologies.
2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Lack of understanding and familiarity with DC technologies among designers, regulators, and permitting authorities can result in lengthy and expensive design and permit processes, which can discourage the adoption of DC technologies.
3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The limited availability of skilled personnel with expertise in DC technologies may hinder the deployment and maintenance of DC systems, potentially slowing down the transition to DC power systems.
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: The lack of clear and consistent regulations related to DC technologies can create uncertainty for investors, developers, and manufacturers, making it difficult to plan and implement DC projects.
5. High costs of DC solutions: The initial costs of DC solutions, such as high-voltage DC transmission and power conversion equipment, may be higher than their AC counterparts, making it challenging to compete with established AC technologies. These five barriers have been identified as key challenges that need to be addressed to accelerate the transition to a sustainable energy system through the use of DC technologies.
