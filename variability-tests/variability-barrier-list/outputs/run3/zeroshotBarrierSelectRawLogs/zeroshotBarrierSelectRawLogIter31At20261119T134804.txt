3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap
5. High costs of DC solutions

These five barriers have been selected based on their potential to significantly impact the transition to a sustainable energy system through the promotion of DC technologies, as outlined in the Shift2DC project context.

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The diversity of DC components across various industries can lead to compatibility issues, which can increase costs and complexity during system design and implementation.

2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: A lack of understanding about DC technologies can result in lengthy and expensive design and permit processes due to the need for education and clarification about the specific requirements and benefits of DC systems.

3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The relatively limited adoption of DC technologies means that there are fewer trained professionals available to design, install, and maintain DC systems, which can slow down the widespread adoption of DC solutions.

4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Regulatory uncertainty can hinder the development and deployment of DC technologies, as it can create obstacles for investors, developers, and manufacturers.

5. High costs of DC solutions: The high initial costs of DC technologies compared to traditional AC systems can be a significant barrier to their widespread adoption, particularly in applications where cost-effectiveness is a critical factor.

The remaining barriers on the list could also be potential barriers to the widespread adoption of DC technologies, but the selected barriers have been chosen based on their relevance to the Shift2DC project's focus on accelerating the transition to a sustainable energy system through the promotion of DC technologies in four sectors: ports, industry, data centers, and buildings.
