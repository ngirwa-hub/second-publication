1. Incompatibility of DC systems components
2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process
3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap
5. High costs of DC solutions

These five barriers have been selected as they directly impact the transition to a sustainable energy system by promoting the use of direct current (DC) technologies, as outlined in the Shift2DC project.

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The lack of standardization and compatibility in DC components can hinder the widespread adoption of DC technologies, making integration complex and costly.

2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Limited understanding of DC technologies can lead to prolonged and expensive design and permit processes, potentially deterring stakeholders from adopting DC solutions.

3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: Insufficient skilled labor in DC technologies can slow down the implementation and maintenance of DC systems, ultimately hindering the transition to a sustainable energy system.

4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Ambiguity in regulations and standards for DC technologies can create uncertainty for investors and project developers, potentially discouraging the adoption of DC solutions.

5. High costs of DC solutions: The higher initial costs of DC technologies compared to alternating current (AC) solutions can be a barrier to widespread adoption, particularly in the context of tight budgets and competing energy priorities.

The remaining barriers, while important, were not selected due to the specific focus on the Shift2DC project and its objectives.
