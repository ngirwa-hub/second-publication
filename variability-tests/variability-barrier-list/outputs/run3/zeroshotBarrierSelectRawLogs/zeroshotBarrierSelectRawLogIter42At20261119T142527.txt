Based on the provided context and my expertise, I have selected the following five barriers from the list that I believe are most relevant to the Shift2DC project and the promotion of DC technologies:

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The lack of standardization and interoperability between various DC components can pose challenges in integrating and optimizing DC systems, which can lead to increased costs and reduced efficiency.
2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Insufficient understanding of DC technologies among designers, engineers, and regulators can lead to lengthy and expensive design and permit processes, hindering the widespread adoption of DC technologies.
3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The limited number of trained professionals familiar with DC technologies can slow down the deployment of DC solutions, as it can be difficult to find qualified personnel to design, install, and maintain DC systems.
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Ambiguity and uncertainty in regulations regarding DC systems can create barriers for the widespread adoption of DC technologies, as it can be difficult for stakeholders to navigate the regulatory landscape and make informed decisions about investing in DC solutions.
5. High costs of DC solutions: The initial costs of DC solutions can be higher than those of AC solutions, due to factors such as the need for specialized components and power conversion equipment. This can make DC technologies less attractive to potential adopters, particularly in the early stages of market development.

These five barriers are critical to address in order to accelerate the transition to a sustainable energy system through the promotion of DC technologies.
