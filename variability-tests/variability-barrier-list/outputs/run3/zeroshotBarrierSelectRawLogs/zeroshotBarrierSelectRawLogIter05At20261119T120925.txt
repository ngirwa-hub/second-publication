Based on the provided context and my expertise in DC technologies, the following are the five barriers I would select from the list:

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: This barrier is significant as it can lead to increased costs and complexity in the design and implementation of DC systems due to the need for specialized components that are compatible with each other.

2. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: As DC technologies are still emerging, there is a shortage of trained personnel who can design, install, and maintain DC systems. This can lead to higher costs and delays in project implementation.

3. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Regulatory uncertainties can create barriers for the widespread adoption of DC technologies. A clear and consistent regulatory framework is necessary to encourage investment in DC technologies.

4. High costs of DC solutions: Compared to AC solutions, DC solutions can be more expensive due to the need for specialized components and infrastructure. This can make it difficult for DC technologies to compete with established AC technologies.

5. Lack of pilot projects: The absence of successful pilot projects can make it difficult to demonstrate the benefits of DC technologies and gain the confidence of investors, policymakers, and the public. Pilot projects are essential for validating the technical and economic feasibility of DC technologies.

The other barriers listed, such as power losses, quality and safety issues, reduced reliability in DC devices, lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous, uncertain utility interaction, and lack of public perception, are also important challenges that need to be addressed to accelerate the transition to a sustainable energy system using DC technologies. However, for the purposes of this expert elicitation exercise, I have selected the five barriers that I believe are most significant based on the provided context and my expertise.
