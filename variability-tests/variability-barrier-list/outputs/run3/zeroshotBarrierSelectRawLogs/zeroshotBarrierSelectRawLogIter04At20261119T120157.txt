3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap
5. High costs of DC solutions

These five barriers have been selected as they present significant challenges in the transition to a sustainable energy system by promoting the use of direct current (DC) technologies.

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The use of various DC components from different manufacturers can lead to compatibility issues, making it challenging to integrate and optimize DC systems.

2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Limited understanding of DC technology can result in lengthy and costly design and permit processes, hindering the widespread adoption of DC solutions.

3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The availability of skilled professionals with expertise in DC technology is essential for the successful implementation and maintenance of DC systems.

4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Clear and consistent regulations are necessary to ensure the growth and stability of the DC market, but the current regulatory landscape can be unclear, making it difficult for stakeholders to invest in DC technologies.

5. High costs of DC solutions: The high initial costs of DC solutions can deter potential adopters, making it challenging to achieve widespread adoption and scale up the use of DC technologies in various sectors.
