These five barriers have been selected based on their potential impact on the transition to a sustainable energy system using direct current (DC) technologies, as outlined in the Shift2DC project.

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The interoperability and compatibility issues among various DC systems components can hinder the widespread adoption of DC technologies.

2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Limited understanding of DC technology can lead to prolonged design and approval processes, adding to the costs and delays in implementing DC solutions.

3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The scarcity of skilled professionals familiar with DC systems can make it challenging to design, install, and maintain DC projects, further slowing the transition to DC technologies.

4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Regulatory uncertainties, such as net metering policies, utility ownership, and agreed standards, can create barriers for the deployment and integration of DC technologies into the existing energy infrastructure.

5. High costs of DC solutions: The initial costs associated with DC technologies can be a significant barrier, making it difficult for projects to compete with established, cheaper alternatives, such as AC-based systems.
