These five barriers have been selected as they directly impact the transition to a sustainable energy system by promoting the use of direct current (DC) technologies, as outlined in the Shift2DC project.

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The complexity of integrating various DC components can lead to compatibility issues, which can increase costs and delay the implementation of DC systems.

2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Misunderstanding of DC technologies can lead to extended design and permit processes, resulting in increased costs and delays in the deployment of DC solutions.

3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: A shortage of skilled workers familiar with DC technologies can hinder the adoption and implementation of DC systems, potentially slowing down the transition to a sustainable energy system.

4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Unclear or inconsistent regulations can create uncertainty for investors and project developers, making it difficult to plan and execute DC projects, and potentially slowing down the transition to a sustainable energy system.

5. High costs of DC solutions: High initial costs associated with DC technologies can deter potential adopters, making it challenging to scale up the use of DC solutions and accelerate the transition to a sustainable energy system.
