--- mistral_generalist ---
1. Incompatibility of DC systems components
2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process
3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap
5. High costs of DC solutions

These five barriers have been selected based on their potential to significantly impact the transition to a sustainable energy system using direct current (DC) technologies, as outlined in the Shift2DC project context.

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: This barrier refers to the challenge of ensuring that various DC components and systems can work seamlessly together, which is essential for efficient and reliable operation.

2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: This barrier highlights the need for better understanding and education about DC technologies, as misconceptions can lead to longer and more expensive design and permit processes.

3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: As DC technologies become more prevalent, there is a growing need for skilled professionals who are well-versed in the design, installation, and maintenance of DC systems.

4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: A clear and consistent regulatory environment is crucial for the widespread adoption of DC technologies. However, an uncertain regulatory roadmap can create uncertainty and hinder investment in DC solutions.

5. High costs of DC solutions: The cost of implementing DC technologies can be a significant barrier, as they often require more expensive components and infrastructure compared to traditional AC systems. Reducing costs and finding ways to make DC solutions more affordable is essential for widespread adoption.

These five barriers have been chosen because addressing them could have a substantial impact on accelerating the transition to a sustainable energy system using DC technologies.

--- mistral_normative ---
1. Incompatibility of DC systems components
2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process
3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap
5. High costs of DC solutions

These five barriers are significant for the acceleration of the transition to a sustainable energy system through the promotion of direct current (DC) technologies, as outlined in the Shift2DC project.

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The heterogeneity of DC components hinders the seamless integration of DC systems, making it challenging to achieve scalability and cost-effectiveness.

2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: The limited understanding of DC technologies among stakeholders, such as policymakers, designers, and regulators, can lead to lengthy and expensive approval processes due to the need for extensive justification and education.

3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The scarcity of skilled professionals familiar with DC technologies can slow down the adoption and implementation of DC solutions, as there may not be enough experts to design, install, and maintain DC systems.

4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: The absence of clear and consistent regulations for DC technologies can create uncertainty for investors, developers, and manufacturers, hindering the growth of the DC market.

5. High costs of DC solutions: The higher initial costs of DC solutions compared to AC systems can discourage potential adopters, making it difficult to achieve widespread adoption and economic viability.

While the following barriers are also important, the selected five are considered the most significant for the acceleration of the transition to a sustainable energy system through the promotion of DC technologies:

6. Power losses, quality and safety issues: Concerns about power losses, quality, and safety associated with DC systems can deter potential adopters and delay the widespread adoption of DC technologies.

7. Reduced reliability in DC devices: The perceived lower reliability of DC devices compared to AC devices can lead to hesitation among potential adopters and create a reluctance to invest in DC solutions.

8. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: The limited number of applications that clearly demonstrate the advantages of DC technologies can make it difficult to convince potential adopters of the benefits of transitioning to DC systems.

9. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): The uncertainty surrounding utility interaction, including net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards, can create barriers for the integration of DC systems into the existing grid infrastructure.

10. Lack of pilot projects: The absence of pilot projects demonstrating the effectiveness and feasibility of DC technologies can make it difficult to gain traction and attract investment in DC solutions.

11. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects: The general public's perception of DC technologies, as well as their willingness to support and advocate for the installation of DC projects, can have a significant impact on the widespread adoption of DC solutions. However, since this barrier is less specific to the technical challenges of DC systems and more related to public attitudes and awareness, it has been excluded from the list of five selected barriers.
