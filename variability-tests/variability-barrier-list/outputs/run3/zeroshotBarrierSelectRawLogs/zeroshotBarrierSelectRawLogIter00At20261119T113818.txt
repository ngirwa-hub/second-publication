Based on the context provided and my expertise in DC technologies, I would select the following five barriers that are most relevant to the Shift2DC project:

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The diversity of DC systems components can lead to compatibility issues, making it challenging to design and implement efficient DC systems.

2. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: As DC technologies are still emerging, there is a shortage of skilled professionals who can design, install, and maintain DC systems, which can slow down the adoption of these technologies.

3. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Unclear regulations and standards for DC systems can create uncertainty and increase the cost of implementation, as companies may need to invest in research and development to meet evolving requirements.

4. High costs of DC solutions: Although the long-term benefits of DC technologies are significant, the initial costs can be prohibitive, making it difficult for businesses and individuals to invest in these solutions.

5. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): The interaction between DC systems and utilities is not yet well-defined, creating uncertainties regarding net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards, which can discourage investments in DC technologies.

These five barriers are critical to address in order to accelerate the transition to a sustainable energy system using DC technologies.
