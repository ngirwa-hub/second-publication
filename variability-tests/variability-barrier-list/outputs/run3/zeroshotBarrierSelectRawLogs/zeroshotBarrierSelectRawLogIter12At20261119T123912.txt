1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The lack of standardization and compatibility between different DC system components can lead to increased complexity, higher costs, and longer timeframes for system deployment.

2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: A lack of understanding and familiarity with DC technologies among designers, permitting authorities, and other stakeholders can result in lengthy and expensive design and permit processes, hindering the adoption of DC solutions.

3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The shortage of skilled professionals with expertise in DC technologies can lead to difficulties in the planning, design, installation, and maintenance of DC systems, further slowing their adoption.

4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: The absence of clear and consistent regulations for DC technologies can create uncertainty and increase risks for investors, developers, and manufacturers, potentially discouraging the growth of the DC market.

5. High costs of DC solutions: The cost of DC technologies can be higher than their AC counterparts, especially when considering the additional components required for DC systems, such as converters and inverters. This higher cost can make DC solutions less attractive to potential adopters compared to traditional technologies.
