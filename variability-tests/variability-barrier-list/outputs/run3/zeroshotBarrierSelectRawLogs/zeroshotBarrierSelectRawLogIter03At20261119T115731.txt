These five barriers are particularly significant in the context of the Shift2DC project, as they directly impact the adoption and implementation of DC technologies in the targeted sectors (ports, industry, data centers, and buildings).

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The lack of standardization and interoperability between various DC components can lead to increased complexity and costs in system design and integration.

2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: A lack of understanding of DC technologies can lead to longer and more expensive design and permit processes, as regulators and permitting authorities may not be familiar with DC systems, requiring additional time and resources to ensure compliance.

3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The limited availability of skilled professionals with expertise in DC technologies can create challenges in the deployment, operation, and maintenance of DC systems.

4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Regulatory uncertainty can hinder the adoption of DC technologies, as it creates risks and uncertainties for investors and project developers.

5. High costs of DC solutions: The higher initial costs of DC solutions compared to AC systems can be a significant barrier to their widespread adoption, particularly in markets with limited budgets or subsidies for renewable energy infrastructure.

The remaining barriers, while important, were not selected for this exercise as they are more closely related to technical challenges and specific use-cases rather than the broader barriers to the widespread adoption of DC technologies in the targeted sectors.
