Based on the provided context and my role as an expert in renewable energy and DC technologies, the following are the five barriers I would select from the list:

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The variety of DC components and their differing voltage levels can create issues with compatibility, which can complicate the integration and deployment of DC systems.

2. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: As DC technologies gain traction, there is a growing need for skilled professionals who can design, install, and maintain DC systems. However, the current workforce may not yet have the necessary expertise.

3. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Regulatory uncertainties can create challenges for the development and deployment of DC technologies, as clear and consistent regulations are essential for the widespread adoption of these technologies.

4. High costs of DC solutions: While DC technologies offer numerous benefits, they can still be more expensive than their AC counterparts, which can make it difficult for projects to secure funding and attract investors.

5. Lack of pilot projects: Demonstrating the effectiveness and efficiency of DC technologies through pilot projects can help build confidence in these technologies and encourage wider adoption. However, the lack of such projects can hinder their growth and development.

The remaining barriers on the list are also important considerations in the transition to DC technologies, but the five barriers listed above align best with the focus and objectives of the Shift2DC project as described in the context provided.
