--- mistral_generalist ---
1. Incompatibility of DC systems components
2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process
3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap
5. High costs of DC solutions

These five barriers have been selected as they are significant challenges in the transition to a sustainable energy system using direct current (DC) technologies.

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The lack of standardization in DC components can lead to compatibility issues, making it difficult and expensive to integrate DC systems into existing infrastructure.

2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: A lack of understanding about DC technology can lead to longer design and permit processes, increasing costs and delaying the transition to DC systems.

3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The limited number of trained professionals in DC systems can slow down the adoption and implementation of DC technologies, as there are not enough people to design, install, and maintain these systems.

4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Regulatory uncertainty can create obstacles for the widespread adoption of DC technologies, as it can make it difficult for companies to invest in new technologies and businesses.

5. High costs of DC solutions: The higher costs of DC solutions compared to AC systems can deter potential adopters, making it difficult to scale up the use of DC technologies in various sectors like ports, industry, data centers, and buildings.
