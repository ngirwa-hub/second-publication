These five barriers have been selected as they directly impact the transition to a sustainable energy system by promoting the use of direct current (DC) technologies. They are significant hurdles in the Shift2DC project's aim to develop and demonstrate DC solutions in ports, industry, data centers, and buildings.

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The diversity and complexity of DC systems components can lead to incompatibility issues, hindering the seamless integration of DC technologies in various sectors.

2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: Limited understanding and misconceptions about DC technologies can result in extended design and permit processes, leading to increased costs and delays in project implementation.

3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The paucity of skilled workers familiar with DC technologies can impede the deployment and maintenance of DC systems, potentially slowing the transition to a more sustainable energy system.

4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: The absence of clear and consistent regulations regarding DC technologies can create uncertainty for investors and project developers, discouraging the adoption of DC solutions.

5. High costs of DC solutions: The higher initial costs associated with DC technologies compared to traditional AC systems can be a significant barrier to widespread adoption, particularly in the context of budget constraints and competing priorities in various sectors.
