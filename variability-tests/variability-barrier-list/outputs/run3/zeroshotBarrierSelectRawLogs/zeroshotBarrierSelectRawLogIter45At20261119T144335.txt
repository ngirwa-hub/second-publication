1. Incompatibility of DC systems components
2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process
3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap
5. High costs of DC solutions

These barriers have been selected based on their direct impact on the adoption and implementation of DC technologies, particularly in the context of the Shift2DC project. The selected barriers address technical, regulatory, and social challenges that need to be addressed for a successful transition to a sustainable energy system using DC technologies.

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: This barrier refers to the challenges associated with integrating different DC components, as well as DC and AC systems, due to a lack of standardization and interoperability issues.

2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: This barrier highlights the need for increased awareness and understanding of DC technologies among designers, regulators, and permitting authorities, to avoid delays and unnecessary expenses during the design and permit process.

3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: As DC technologies become more prevalent, there is a need for a skilled workforce to design, install, and maintain DC systems. The lack of trained personnel can lead to delays, increased costs, and reduced efficiency in the adoption of DC technologies.

4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Regulatory uncertainty can create barriers for the widespread adoption of DC technologies. A clear and supportive regulatory environment is essential for the successful implementation of DC solutions in various sectors, such as ports, industry, data centers, and buildings.

5. High costs of DC solutions: The high initial costs of DC solutions can be a significant barrier to their widespread adoption. Reducing costs and demonstrating the economic viability of DC technologies is crucial for their acceptance and integration into various sectors.
