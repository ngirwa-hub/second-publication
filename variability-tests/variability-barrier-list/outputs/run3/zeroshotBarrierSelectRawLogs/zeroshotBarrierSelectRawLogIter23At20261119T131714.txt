2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process
3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap
5. High costs of DC solutions

These barriers were chosen due to their potential significant impact on the acceleration of the transition to a sustainable energy system by promoting the use of direct current (DC) technologies. Incompatibility of DC systems components can hinder the efficient integration and interoperability of DC systems, making it difficult to scale up projects. Misconception and lack of knowledge can lead to additional costs and delays in design and permit processes. A lack of trained personnel can slow down the deployment and maintenance of DC systems. An uncertain regulatory roadmap can create uncertainty for investors and developers, making it harder to secure funding for DC projects. High costs of DC solutions can make them less competitive compared to traditional AC systems, potentially limiting their adoption.
