These five barriers were selected as they represent significant challenges in the transition to DC technologies, as outlined by the Shift2DC project.

1. Incompatibility of DC systems components: The variety of DC components and systems can lead to compatibility issues, making it difficult to design and implement efficient and effective DC systems.

2. Misconception and lack of knowledge leads to lengthy/expensive design and permit process: A lack of understanding about DC technologies can result in longer and more expensive design and permit processes, as regulators and stakeholders may require additional information or testing to ensure safety and compliance.

3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: As DC technologies become more prevalent, there is a need for a skilled workforce to design, install, and maintain these systems. However, the current workforce may not be adequately trained in DC technologies, making it difficult to deploy and manage these systems effectively.

4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Regulatory uncertainty can create challenges for the development and deployment of DC technologies, as it can be difficult to predict how regulations will evolve and how they will impact the cost and feasibility of DC projects.

5. High costs of DC solutions: The initial costs of DC technologies can be higher than those of traditional AC systems, which can make it difficult to justify the investment in DC technologies, particularly in the early stages of deployment.
