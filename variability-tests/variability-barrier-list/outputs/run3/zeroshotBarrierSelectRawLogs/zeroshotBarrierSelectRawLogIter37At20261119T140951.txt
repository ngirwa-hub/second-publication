3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems
4. Uncertain regulatory roadmap
5. High costs of DC solutions

These five barriers have been selected as they present significant challenges in the transition to a sustainable energy system using direct current (DC) technologies. Incompatibility of DC systems components can lead to technical difficulties and increased costs. Misconception and lack of knowledge can lead to lengthy and expensive design and permit processes, potentially slowing down the adoption of DC technologies. The lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems can hinder the efficient implementation and maintenance of DC systems. An uncertain regulatory roadmap can create uncertainty for investors and project developers, potentially deterring them from investing in DC technologies. Lastly, high costs of DC solutions can make them less appealing to potential adopters, especially in the early stages of the transition to a sustainable energy system.
