--- mistral-subject-matter ---
- lack of pilot projects
- reduced reliability in DC devices
- lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems
- uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards)
- high costs of DC solutions

These five barriers are critical for the accelerated transition to a sustainable energy system through the promotion of direct current (DC) technologies.

1. Lack of pilot projects: The absence of successful demonstration projects hinders the adoption of DC technologies, making it difficult for stakeholders to understand the benefits and feasibility of DC solutions.

2. Reduced reliability in DC devices: The reliability of DC devices is often perceived as lower compared to AC devices. This concern can deter potential adopters and slow down the transition to DC technologies.

3. Lack of enough trained personnel in DC systems: The scarcity of skilled professionals familiar with DC systems makes it challenging to implement and maintain DC projects effectively, thereby increasing costs and delays.

4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): The uncertain regulatory framework for DC technologies can lead to unclear expectations regarding net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards, which can create barriers for the deployment of DC solutions.

5. High costs of DC solutions: The initial investment costs for DC technologies are often higher than those for AC systems. This financial barrier can discourage potential adopters, especially in the early stages of technology deployment.
