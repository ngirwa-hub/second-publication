- incompatibility of DC systems components

These five barriers have been selected as they directly address some of the main challenges faced in the transition to a sustainable energy system using direct current (DC) technologies. Power losses, quality, and safety issues are important concerns due to the differences between AC and DC systems. High costs of DC solutions can make it difficult for projects to be financially viable. An uncertain regulatory roadmap can create uncertainty for investors and project developers. Uncertain utility interaction can also pose challenges, particularly around net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards. Lastly, incompatibility of DC systems components can hinder the widespread adoption of DC technologies.

--- mistral-subject-matter ---
- power losses, quality and safety issues
- high costs of DC solutions
- uncertain regulatory roadmap
- uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards)
- incompatibility of DC systems components

These five barriers are the most relevant to the context of the Shift2DC project, as they directly impact the adoption and implementation of DC technologies in the four sectors (ports, industry, data centers, and buildings) that the project focuses on.

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues are crucial concerns in DC power systems due to the potential for higher voltage levels and the need for specialized components.

2. High costs of DC solutions are a significant barrier as they may not always be competitive with AC systems, especially in the initial stages of adoption.

3. An uncertain regulatory roadmap can hinder the growth of DC technologies, as clear guidelines are necessary for market penetration and investment.

4. Uncertain utility interaction, including issues with net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards, can create challenges in integrating DC solutions into the existing grid infrastructure.

5. Incompatibility of DC systems components is a technical challenge that can lead to increased costs and complexity during system design and implementation.
