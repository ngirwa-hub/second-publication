These barriers are significant in the transition to DC technologies as they pose challenges in terms of cost, compatibility, skills, safety, and regulatory frameworks. High costs of DC solutions may deter adoption, while incompatibility of components can hinder system integration. A lack of trained personnel can slow down the deployment and maintenance of DC systems, and power losses, quality, and safety issues can impact the efficiency and reliability of DC devices. An uncertain regulatory roadmap can create uncertainty for investors and project developers, and lack of use-cases can make it difficult to demonstrate the advantages of DC over traditional AC systems. Uncertain utility interaction can also complicate the integration of DC systems into the existing grid infrastructure.
