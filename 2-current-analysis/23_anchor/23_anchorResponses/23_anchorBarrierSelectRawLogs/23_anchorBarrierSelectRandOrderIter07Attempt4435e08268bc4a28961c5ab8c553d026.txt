These barriers represent some of the key challenges that need to be addressed to facilitate the widespread adoption of medium- and low-voltage DC solutions. The regulatory environment, the lack of proven use-cases, uncertainties in utility interactions, high costs, and misconceptions about DC technology can all hinder the growth of DC-powered systems. Addressing these barriers will be crucial for the successful implementation and integration of DC technologies in various sectors, such as data centers, buildings, industry, and ports.

These barriers are significant challenges in the widespread adoption of medium- and low-voltage DC technologies. Uncertain regulatory roadmaps create ambiguity for investors and project developers, while a lack of use-cases where DC is advantageous limits the appeal of DC solutions. Uncertain utility interaction can create barriers to net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards, which are crucial for the integration of DC grids. High costs of DC solutions can make them less competitive compared to traditional AC systems, and misconceptions and lack of knowledge can lead to lengthy and expensive design and permit processes.
