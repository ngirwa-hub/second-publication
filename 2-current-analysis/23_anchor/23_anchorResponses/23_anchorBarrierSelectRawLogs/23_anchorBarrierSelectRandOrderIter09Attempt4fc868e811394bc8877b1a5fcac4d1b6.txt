These barriers represent potential challenges that may hinder the widespread adoption of medium- and low-voltage DC technologies. Power losses, quality, and safety issues can impact the efficiency and reliability of DC systems. Misconceptions and lack of knowledge can lead to complex and costly design and permit processes. High costs of DC solutions can make them less competitive compared to AC systems. Uncertain utility interaction and regulatory issues can create challenges in integrating DC systems into the existing grid infrastructure. An uncertain regulatory roadmap can create uncertainty for investors and project developers. Lack of pilot projects and trained personnel can hinder the development and implementation of DC systems. Lastly, the lack of use-cases where DC is advantageous can make it difficult to demonstrate the benefits of DC technologies to potential adopters.
