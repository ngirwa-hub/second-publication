SHIFT2DC Expert Elicitation - Session 1
Fri, Oct 25, 2024

Summary:

The meeting focused on the expert elicitation process related to low and medium voltage technologies within the context of the EU-funded DC shift project. Hartwig Stamberger expressed interest in understanding this process, while Lucas Pereira outlined the meeting's objectives, which included gathering insights from approximately ten experts across various sectors, such as ports and data centers. The agenda featured introductions, an overview of the project, and a detailed explanation of the expert elicitation protocol, all aimed at informing future discussions with users.

Participants introduced themselves and shared their backgrounds in DC projects and research. Hartwig Stamberger from ETAN discussed his coordination of a large-scale DC initiative and his leadership role in the Open DC Alliance. Gen Li from DTU highlighted his focus on HVDC operation and offshore wind energy, while Rama P from Schneider Eritrea emphasized his leadership in DC projects. Dominik Maihöfner represented the Open Alliance, which advocates for DC applications, and other participants contributed insights into energy efficiency and DC installations.

Lucas Pereira provided an overview of the CFTC project, which aims to establish guidelines for the application of DC technologies, particularly in low and medium voltage contexts. He discussed the development of various tools and solutions with a focus on sustainability and user-friendliness. Additionally, he introduced physical demonstrators being set up for testing technologies, including a micro data center in Germany and a small installation at a port, to gather user perspectives and test technologies in real-world settings.

The discussion highlighted the importance of addressing barriers to technology adoption, particularly in developing countries. Lucas Pereira emphasized the need for insights on feasibility, barriers, and costs, while participants identified issues such as the lack of trained personnel and societal resistance to change. The conversation also touched on the significance of education and comprehensive training in energy management, as well as the bureaucratic challenges faced in energy exchange and compliance. Overall, the meeting underscored the collaborative effort required to advance DC technologies and the importance of expert insights in shaping future initiatives.


Chapters & Topics:

Expert Elicitation Discussion
Hartwig Stamberger joined the meeting while traveling and inquired about the expert elicitation process. Lucas Pereira clarified that the goal is to gather expert opinions on low and medium voltage technologies as part of the EU-funded DC shift project. The meeting is designed to facilitate discussions among experts to reach a consensus on specific technologies and challenges.
* Overview of the Shift to DC project and its focus on low and medium voltage technologies.

Participant Introductions and Backgrounds
Participants introduced themselves, sharing their affiliations and areas of expertise. Hartwig Stamberger from ETAN discussed his coordination of a significant DC project and his leadership role in the Open DC Alliance. Gen Li from DTU mentioned his research on HVDC control and offshore wind energy, while Rama P from Schneider Eritrea highlighted his work on DC projects. Dominik Maihöfner represented the Open Alliance, which promotes DC applications among its 72 member companies.

Overview of CFTC Project and Development of DC Solutions
The CFTC project, led by Lucas Pereira, is focused on advancing the application of DC technologies through the development of 24 solutions, including smart and sustainable DC cables and connectors. Key components include a solid-state circuit breaker with real-time monitoring capabilities and a low voltage AC-DC interlink converter. The project emphasizes user-friendly design tools to facilitate community engagement and ease of use.
* Discussion of specific technologies being developed, including smart and sustainable DC cables, connectors, solid-state circuit breakers, and interlink converters.

Project Demonstrators and Trials Overview
Lucas Pereira outlined the project’s physical demonstrators, including a micro data center in Germany and a small installation at a port, which will feature technologies like solar panels and V2G chargers. PT Dr. Stephan Rupp inquired about the nature of these installations, confirming that they will be real setups rather than just simulations. The goal is to test technologies and gather insights from end users, including tourists at the port.

Expert Elicitation on DC Solutions
The discussion led by Lucas Pereira aimed to gather expert insights on the challenges and opportunities related to the adoption of DC solutions. Dr. Stephan Rupp raised concerns about the common assumption that acceptance is the primary barrier, suggesting that readiness of technology might also be a significant factor. Participants were encouraged to engage through Slido and share their perspectives on feasibility and importance of the proposed solutions.

Discussion on the Importance and Feasibility of DC Technology Solutions
Lucas Pereira facilitated a conversation regarding the importance of different DC technology solutions, questioning whether the absence of certain solutions would hinder progress. Hartwig Stammberger and Dominik Maihöfner contributed insights on the necessity of smart cables versus existing technologies, highlighting that while cables are essential, their smart designation may be overstated. The discussion also touched on the relevance of solutions based on geographical context.

Barriers to Technology Adoption in Developing Regions
Lucas Pereira led a discussion on the barriers to widespread technology adoption, asking participants to select the top five challenges. Key issues identified included a lack of trained personnel, uncertainty, and misconceptions. Yannick Neyret emphasized the need for more training programs and collaboration with universities to address the skills gap.

Discussion on Challenges and Costs of DC Technology
Lucas Pereira initiated a discussion on the cost implications of various DC solutions, noting that initial market prices could be prohibitive. Hartwig Stammberger agreed, stating that small series products tend to be more expensive than mass-produced items, which could hinder project initiation. He suggested that national or EU bodies might need to address these cost challenges.

Cost Implications and Challenges in DC Project Installations
Lucas Pereira and Rama P addressed the cost implications of converters in DC projects, noting that manufacturing scale and modularity can significantly influence project decisions. PT Dr. Stephan Rupp pointed out that while converter costs are low, the lack of expertise and interoperability with existing systems often leads to higher overall project costs. Yannick Neyret added that mass production and improved education for installers are crucial for making DC solutions more competitive.

Discussion on Education, Government Support, and AC/DC Transition
Lucas Pereira highlighted the significance of education and resilience in their discussions, mentioning the need for calibration of answers. Hartwig Stammberger participated briefly before departing, and the conversation shifted to the relevance of government support for large-scale DC technology installations. Boris Sučić suggested that state support could be beneficial for specific installations, while PT Dr. Stephan Rupp argued against the necessity of government subsidies.
* Government support and incentives for DC technology adoption.

Discussion on Technology Adoption and Control Systems
Lucas Pereira stressed the importance of foundational training and robust energy management systems to effectively handle hybrid technologies. Boris Sučić shared his perspective on the bureaucratic hurdles in Slovenia that complicate energy exchanges and compliance with grid codes. Both speakers acknowledged the need for more pilot projects and data sharing to facilitate progress in the sector.
* Bureaucratic challenges in implementing new technologies and pilot projects.
