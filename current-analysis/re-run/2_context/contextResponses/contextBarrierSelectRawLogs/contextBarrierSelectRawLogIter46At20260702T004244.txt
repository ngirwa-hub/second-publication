1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues
2. Reduced reliability in DC devices
3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous
4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards)
5. Lack of pilot projects

These barriers represent significant challenges that need to be addressed for the widespread adoption of medium- and low-voltage DC technologies. Power losses, quality, and safety issues are crucial concerns due to the nature of direct current and its impact on energy transmission and distribution. Reduced reliability in DC devices is a concern because it can lead to system failures and downtime, affecting the overall efficiency of DC solutions.

Lack of use-cases where DC is advantageous is an important barrier as it demonstrates the need for more evidence and real-world examples to showcase the benefits of DC technologies over traditional AC systems. Uncertain utility interaction is a critical issue that needs resolution, especially regarding net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards, to ensure smooth integration of DC solutions into existing grid infrastructures. Lastly, the lack of pilot projects is significant as they provide valuable insights into the practicality and effectiveness of DC technologies, helping to build confidence in their adoption.
