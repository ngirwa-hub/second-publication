--- generalist_mistral ---
Based on my expertise, I would select the following five barriers from the list:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: The higher voltage levels in DC grids can lead to increased power losses due to resistance in cables and components. Ensuring power quality and maintaining safety standards are crucial concerns that need to be addressed.

2. Reduced reliability in DC devices: Some DC devices may have lower reliability compared to their AC counterparts, which could impact the overall performance and stability of the DC grid.

3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: While there are potential benefits to using DC technologies, it's essential to demonstrate practical applications where they provide clear advantages over existing AC solutions.

4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): Clear guidelines for net metering, utility ownership, and standardization are necessary to ensure a smooth integration of DC grids into the existing power infrastructure.

5. Lack of pilot projects: Pilot projects help validate new technologies and demonstrate their feasibility in real-world conditions. The absence of enough pilot projects can hinder the widespread adoption of DC solutions.

--- normative_mistral ---
Based on my role as a normative expert in renewable energy and direct current (DC) technologies, I would select the following five barriers for discussion:

1. power losses, quality and safety issues
2. reduced reliability in DC devices
3. uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards)
4. lack of pilot projects
5. high costs of DC solutions

These barriers are crucial factors that need to be addressed for the widespread adoption of medium- and low-voltage DC technologies. Power losses, quality, and safety issues can impact the efficiency and effectiveness of DC systems, while reduced reliability in DC devices can lead to system failures and downtime. Uncertain utility interaction and a lack of pilot projects create uncertainty and risks that discourage investors and stakeholders from adopting DC solutions. Lastly, high costs are a significant barrier as they make it challenging for many organizations to implement DC technologies, especially for smaller-scale projects like the ones described in the Shift2DC project.
