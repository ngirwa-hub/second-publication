--- generalist_mistral ---
Based on my expert role, I would select the following five barriers for discussion:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: The concern over power losses and quality in DC systems is a significant barrier due to the potential impact on efficiency and reliability. Ensuring safe operations is also crucial for widespread adoption.

2. Reduced reliability in DC devices: Reliability is a key factor in any technology's success, and if DC devices are perceived as less reliable than their AC counterparts, it could hinder the growth of DC systems.

3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: Demonstrating clear benefits over AC technologies will be essential for convincing users to adopt DC solutions. Identifying and promoting successful use cases can help address this barrier.

4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): Establishing clear guidelines for utility companies regarding net metering, ownership rights, and standardization is necessary to ensure a smooth transition towards widespread DC adoption.

5. Lack of pilot projects: Real-world examples of successful DC implementations can help build confidence in the technology and showcase its potential benefits. Increasing the number of pilot projects will be crucial for overcoming this barrier.
