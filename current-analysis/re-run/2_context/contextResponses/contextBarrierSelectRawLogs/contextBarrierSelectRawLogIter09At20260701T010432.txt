--- generalist_mistral ---
Based on my role as an expert in renewable energy and direct current (DC) technologies, I would select the following five barriers to the widespread adoption of medium- and low-voltage DC:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: DC systems can suffer from higher power losses due to voltage drop in long conductor runs. Ensuring quality and safety in these systems is crucial for their successful implementation.

2. Reduced reliability in DC devices: Compared to AC devices, some DC components may have lower reliability, which can impact the overall performance and efficiency of DC systems.

3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: Identifying and demonstrating applications where DC offers clear benefits over traditional AC systems is essential for gaining widespread acceptance and adoption.

4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): Clarifying the role of utilities in DC systems, including net metering policies and standardization, is crucial to ensuring a smooth transition to DC technologies.

5. Lack of pilot projects: Successful demonstration of DC systems through pilot projects can help build confidence among stakeholders, address technical challenges, and provide valuable real-world data for further improvements.
