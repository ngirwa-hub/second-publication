--- subject-matter_mistral ---
Based on my expert role, I would select the following five barriers from the list provided:

1. Power losses, quality and safety issues - The concern about power losses, quality, and safety is a significant barrier to the adoption of DC systems due to the potential for voltage fluctuations, arcing, and other hazards that can compromise system reliability and pose safety risks.

2. Reduced reliability in DC devices - The reduced reliability of DC devices compared to AC devices is another concern. This issue may stem from a lack of maturity in DC components or insufficient testing and validation methodologies for these technologies.

3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous - There are still limited use-cases where DC solutions offer clear advantages over AC systems, such as lower conversion losses, simpler design, and higher power density. Identifying and demonstrating successful applications will help drive wider adoption of DC technologies.

4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards) - The unclear regulatory framework surrounding net metering, utility ownership, and standardization for DC systems can create uncertainty and hinder the growth of the market. Establishing clear guidelines will help pave the way for wider adoption.

5. Lack of pilot projects - A lack of pilot projects demonstrating the benefits and feasibility of DC solutions is another barrier to their widespread adoption. Successful pilot projects can serve as proof-of-concept, helping to build confidence in the technology among stakeholders.
