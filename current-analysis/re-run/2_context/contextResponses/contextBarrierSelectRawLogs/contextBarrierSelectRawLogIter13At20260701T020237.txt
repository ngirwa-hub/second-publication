--- subject-matter_mistral ---
Based on my expertise, I would select the following five barriers:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: High power losses can occur due to the resistance in DC cables, which can lead to reduced efficiency and increased costs. Ensuring quality and safety is crucial to minimize these losses and maintain a reliable system.

2. Reduced reliability in DC devices: Compared to AC systems, DC components may have lower reliability due to factors such as component aging, temperature effects, and the need for more complex power electronics. Improving the reliability of DC devices is essential for widespread adoption.

3. Lack of use-cases where DC is advantageous: Demonstrating clear benefits over traditional AC systems is necessary to motivate the adoption of DC technologies. Identifying and promoting successful applications will help build momentum for wider implementation.

4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): Establishing clear guidelines for net metering, utility ownership, and standardization is essential to ensure a smooth integration of DC systems into the existing grid infrastructure. Without these guidelines, there may be uncertainty regarding costs, responsibilities, and potential conflicts between stakeholders.

5. Lack of pilot projects: Pilot projects provide valuable opportunities to test, validate, and demonstrate the viability of new technologies in real-world settings. The absence of pilot projects can hinder the development and adoption of DC solutions due to a lack of tangible evidence supporting their benefits.
