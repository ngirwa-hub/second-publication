Based on my expertise, I would select the following five barriers for discussion:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: The concern about power losses, quality, and safety is a significant barrier to widespread adoption of DC technologies due to potential risks associated with voltage levels, system design, and maintenance.

2. Reduced reliability in DC devices: DC systems may be more susceptible to failures or reduced efficiency compared to AC systems, leading to increased downtime and maintenance costs. This barrier needs to be addressed to ensure the long-term viability of DC solutions.

3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: Demonstrating the advantages of DC technologies over traditional AC systems can help build support for their widespread adoption. Identifying industries or applications where DC offers clear benefits can help drive growth in this area.

4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): Collaboration between utilities, regulators, and technology providers is essential to establish clear guidelines for net metering, utility ownership, and standardization of DC technologies. This will help ensure the seamless integration of DC systems into existing infrastructure.

5. Lack of pilot projects: The development and testing of pilot projects are crucial for validating the feasibility and performance of DC solutions in real-world scenarios. Successful pilot projects can build confidence in the technology and pave the way for broader adoption.

These five barriers have been identified as significant challenges to the widespread adoption of medium- and low-voltage DC technologies, and addressing them will be crucial for the success of projects like Shift2DC.
