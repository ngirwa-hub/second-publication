Based on my role as an expert in renewable energy and DC technologies, I would select the following five barriers:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: The concern revolves around energy losses during transmission in DC systems, ensuring high-quality power, and maintaining safety standards for users and equipment.
2. Reduced reliability in DC devices: There might be concerns regarding the long-term durability and reliability of DC components compared to AC ones, potentially leading to increased maintenance costs and downtime.
3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: A limited number of applications where DC technology proves to be more beneficial than traditional AC systems could hinder its widespread adoption.
4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): The relationship between DC users and utilities, including net metering policies, utility ownership, and standardization, is crucial for the growth of DC solutions in various applications.
5. Lack of pilot projects: Limited real-world demonstrations and pilot projects can make it difficult to evaluate the performance, cost-effectiveness, and scalability of DC technologies, leading to hesitance among potential users and investors.
