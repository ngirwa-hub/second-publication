--- mistral-generalist ---
Based on my role as an expert in renewable energy and direct current (DC) technologies, I would select the following five barriers:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: DC systems can have lower power losses compared to AC, but ensuring quality and safety is crucial for widespread adoption. Addressing these concerns will be essential for making DC technology more attractive for various applications.

2. Reduced reliability in DC devices: Reliability is a significant concern for any energy system, and it's especially important for DC technologies given their potential advantages over AC systems. Enhancing the reliability of DC devices will help increase confidence in DC solutions.

3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: Developing more demonstrable benefits and clear use-cases for DC technology can help accelerate its adoption. Highlighting scenarios where DC offers tangible advantages over AC systems will be essential for driving wider acceptance.

4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): Collaboration between utilities, regulators, and technology developers is vital to ensure that DC solutions can seamlessly integrate with existing infrastructure. Establishing clear guidelines and agreements around net metering, utility ownership, and standards will be crucial for enabling the widespread adoption of DC technologies.

5. Lack of pilot projects: Real-world demonstrations and pilot projects are essential for validating the performance and feasibility of new technologies. Increasing the number of successful pilot projects can help build confidence in DC solutions and facilitate their wider deployment.
