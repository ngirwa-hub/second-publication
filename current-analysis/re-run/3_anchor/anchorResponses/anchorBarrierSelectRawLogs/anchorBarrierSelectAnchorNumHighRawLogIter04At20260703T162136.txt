As an expert in renewable energy and direct current (DC) technologies, I have carefully considered the provided context and barriers to adoption. Based on my expertise, I have identified five key barriers that impact the widespread adoption of medium- and low-voltage DC systems:

1. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous - Insufficient demonstrations showcasing the benefits of DC systems may hinder their adoption by potential users who are not aware of the advantages it offers over traditional AC solutions.
2. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards) - Confusion surrounding net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards can create barriers for DC adoption, as these aspects need to be clearly defined and regulated by authorities.
3. Lack of trained personnel in DC systems - Insufficient training programs or certifications may result in a shortage of professionals with the necessary expertise to design, install, and maintain DC systems, hindering their widespread adoption.
4. High costs of DC solutions - DC technologies often come with higher initial investment costs compared to traditional AC solutions, which can be a deterrent for potential users who are looking for cost-effective alternatives.
5. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects - Negative public perception or lack of knowledge about the benefits of DC systems may result in hesitancy among potential users, discouraging them from adopting these technologies. Additionally, the absence of successful pilot projects showcasing the advantages of DC can hinder its acceptance by end-users.
