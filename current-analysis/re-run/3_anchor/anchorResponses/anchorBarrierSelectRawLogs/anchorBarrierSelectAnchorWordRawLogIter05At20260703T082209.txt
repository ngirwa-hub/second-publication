Based on the provided context, I would select the following five barriers for discussion:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: As DC systems become more prevalent, ensuring they operate safely and efficiently is crucial. Addressing power loss concerns can help optimize energy utilization and improve overall system reliability.

2. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: Establishing clear and compelling examples where DC technologies offer significant benefits over AC systems will drive adoption among various sectors, such as data centers, buildings, industry, and ports.

3. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): The role of utilities in the transition to DC grids must be defined and standardized. Net metering policies, utility ownership, and established standards will impact the economic viability and widespread adoption of medium- and low-voltage DC technologies.

4. Lack of pilot projects: Real-world demonstrations can help validate the feasibility and potential benefits of DC solutions, providing tangible evidence to support wider deployment across various sectors.

5. Uncertain regulatory roadmap: Clear regulations and guidelines are essential for ensuring a smooth transition to DC systems. A predictable regulatory environment will provide investors with confidence and promote innovation in the field.
