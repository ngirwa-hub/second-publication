1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues - The concern about power losses and the quality of DC systems is a significant barrier due to potential energy waste and safety risks associated with voltage spikes or other malfunctions.
2. Reduced reliability in DC devices - The perceived lack of reliability in DC devices compared to their AC counterparts can lead to hesitance in adopting DC technologies for widespread use.
3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous - There needs to be a clear demonstration of the benefits and advantages of using DC over AC in various applications, as this will help drive adoption and interest in DC technology.
4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards) - Clarifying the role of utilities in the transition to DC systems, including net metering policies, utility ownership, and standardization, is essential for fostering a conducive environment for widespread adoption.
5. Lack of pilot projects - The absence of successful pilot projects demonstrating the practicality and efficiency of DC technology can make it difficult for stakeholders to invest in and adopt these solutions.
