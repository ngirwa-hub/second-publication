The DC solution design tool is pivotal because it directly supports the effective integration and optimization of medium- and low-voltage DC technologies, which can lead to significant distribution-efficiency improvements as noted in peer-reviewed studies. By providing a platform that aligns with both Current-OS and ODCA frameworks and emphasizes usability through features like drag-and-drop functionality, this tool not only aids engineers and designers in conceptualizing and implementing efficient DC systems but also ensures these systems are designed to meet current standards and future scalability needs.

Moreover, considering the early stage of the Shift2DC initiative and the focus on developing guidelines and roadmaps for widespread adoption of DC solutions, a design tool that can accurately model, simulate, and analyze different configurations and scenarios becomes invaluable. It not only aids in reducing time-to-market for new technologies but also significantly lowers the barriers to entry by simplifying complex processes into more manageable tasks. This is particularly important given the project's emphasis on engaging with experts across various sectors (ports, buildings, manufacturing, utilities) to identify challenges and opportunities, as well as addressing uncertainties and mixed views in current literature.

Furthermore, the ability of the tool to support techno-economic analysis ensures that stakeholders can make informed decisions regarding the economic viability and environmental impact of proposed DC solutions. This aligns with the broader goals of enhancing social acceptance, minimizing environmental impacts, and ensuring sustainability within the realm of renewable energy systems powered by direct current technologies. Therefore, in light of its potential to streamline the design process, facilitate compliance with industry standards, support economic and environmental analysis, and ultimately contribute to the successful adoption of DC microgrids, the DC solution design tool is rated as very important (4) in the context of advancing medium- and low-voltage DC technologies.
