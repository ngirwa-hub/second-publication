Based on the Shift2DC project, I have selected five barriers that are relevant to the promotion of direct current (DC) technologies:

1. Power losses, quality and safety issues - This refers to challenges in maintaining efficiency and ensuring reliable operation for DC systems, particularly in high-voltage applications where power losses can be significant.
2. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous - One challenge is identifying sectors or applications where the benefits of using DC technologies outweigh traditional AC solutions, such as reduced efficiency and increased complexity.
3. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards) - Conflicting regulations and standards between utilities can hinder progress in implementing DC technology in different sectors.
4. Lack of pilot projects - Insufficient demonstration projects may discourage adoption by potential users who want to see real-world examples of successful DC implementation.
5. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects - The acceptance and willingness of stakeholders to adopt and promote DC technologies can be a significant barrier, as public perception may not yet fully understand the benefits or be ready to champion new installation projects.
