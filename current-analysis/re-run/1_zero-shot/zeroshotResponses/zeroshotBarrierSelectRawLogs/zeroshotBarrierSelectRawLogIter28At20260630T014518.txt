Based on the provided context, here are five barriers that I would select for the Shift2DC project:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: Direct current (DC) technologies may suffer from higher power losses due to the resistance in conductors, which can negatively impact efficiency and increase costs. Additionally, ensuring quality and safety in DC systems is crucial to prevent accidents and maintain system reliability.

2. Reduced reliability in DC devices: The reliability of DC devices might be lower compared to alternating current (AC) devices due to factors such as component aging, manufacturing defects, or operational failures. Ensuring the reliability of DC systems is essential for their wide-scale adoption.

3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: There may not be enough compelling reasons or applications that demonstrate the clear benefits of using DC technologies over AC, making it challenging to convince stakeholders to invest in DC infrastructure.

4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): The relationship between utilities and DC projects can be complex due to factors such as net metering policies, utility ownership of distribution networks, and the need for standardized protocols. Clarifying these issues is essential for the successful integration of DC technologies into the energy system.

5. Lack of pilot projects: A limited number of successful pilot projects showcasing the benefits and feasibility of DC technologies can hinder their widespread adoption, as they provide valuable evidence and real-world examples that help build confidence in the technology among stakeholders.
