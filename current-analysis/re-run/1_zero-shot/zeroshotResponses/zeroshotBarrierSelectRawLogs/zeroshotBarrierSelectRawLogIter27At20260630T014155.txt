1. Power losses, quality and safety issues - This refers to concerns about energy efficiency and potential risks in using DC systems. Addressing these issues is essential for increasing public acceptance and encouraging wider adoption of DC solutions.
2. Reduced reliability in DC devices - Ensuring the reliability of DC devices is crucial for building trust and demonstrating their capabilities in various sectors, such as ports, industry, data centers, and buildings. Reliability issues can hinder the transition to a sustainable energy system based on DC technologies.
3. Lack of use-cases where DC is advantageous - To accelerate the adoption of DC technologies, it's essential to identify practical scenarios where using DC systems offers clear advantages over traditional AC solutions. Demonstrating these benefits can help build support and encourage investment in DC technologies.
4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards) - Confusion about how utilities will interact with DC-based energy systems is a barrier to widespread adoption. Clear guidelines and agreements on net metering, utility ownership, and standardized protocols can help alleviate these concerns.
5. Lack of pilot projects - To showcase the potential of DC technologies and gain public acceptance, demonstration projects or pilot initiatives are necessary. These projects can help overcome barriers related to safety, reliability, and use-cases while providing valuable experience for future adoption.
