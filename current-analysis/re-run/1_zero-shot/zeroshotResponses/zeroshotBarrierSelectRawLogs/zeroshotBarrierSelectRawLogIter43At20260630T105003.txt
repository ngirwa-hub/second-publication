2. Reduced reliability in DC devices - Some DC components are more prone to failure than their AC counterparts, which can affect the overall reliability of a DC system.
3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous - There needs to be a better understanding of where and why DC technology is advantageous over AC, as this will help drive its adoption in various sectors.
4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards) - Clear guidelines and standards for the integration of DC systems with existing grid infrastructure are necessary to ensure seamless operation and avoid conflicts with utilities.
5. Lack of pilot projects - Successful demonstration of DC technology in real-world applications can help build confidence and encourage wider adoption among stakeholders.
