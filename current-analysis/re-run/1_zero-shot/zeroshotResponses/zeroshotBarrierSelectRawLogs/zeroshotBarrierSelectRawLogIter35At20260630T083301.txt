These five barriers have been selected as they represent significant challenges to the widespread adoption of direct current (DC) technologies in various sectors, such as ports, industry, data centers, and buildings. Power losses, quality, and safety issues are crucial concerns due to the potential for increased energy loss in DC systems compared to alternating current (AC) systems. Uncertain utility interaction is a barrier because clear standards and guidelines are necessary for effective integration of DC technologies into existing power grids. Lack of use-cases highlights the need for demonstration projects showcasing the benefits of DC technology. Incompatibility of components is a technical challenge that can hinder the scalability of DC systems, while uncertain regulatory roadmaps pose risks to long-term investment in DC solutions. Lastly, insufficient training and knowledge among personnel may lead to lengthy design processes and increased costs for DC projects.
