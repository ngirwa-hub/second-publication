--- llama_subject-matter ---
Based on the provided context and my expertise in renewable energy and direct current (DC) technologies, I have selected five barriers that are most relevant to the Shift2DC project:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues - High-voltage DC transmission can lead to increased power losses due to resistance in the cables or transformers, which may affect efficiency and overall system performance. Moreover, ensuring high-quality components and maintaining safety requirements are essential for successful implementation of DC systems.
2. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous - While DC systems offer several benefits such as increased efficiency, simpler electrical circuits, and reduced cabling costs, there may be limited applications where these advantages outweigh the challenges. Identifying suitable use-cases for DC technology can help promote its adoption and implementation.
3. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards) - DC systems require clear guidelines on net metering, utility ownership, and standardized agreements to ensure smooth integration with existing grid infrastructure. Without proper regulations and agreements in place, implementing DC solutions can face significant obstacles.
4. Lack of pilot projects - Insufficient demonstration projects or real-world examples can hinder the adoption of DC technology. Establishing pilot projects to showcase the practicality and viability of these systems is crucial for gaining acceptance among stakeholders and promoting wider implementation.
5. Public perception of DC and readiness to 'champion' installations from DC projects - Positive public perception and preparedness among industry leaders to champion DC technology are essential for the successful implementation of these systems. Addressing misconceptions, educating the public about the benefits of DC solutions, and fostering a supportive environment can help overcome this barrier.
