--- mistral_subject-matter ---
Based on my role as a subject-matter expert in renewable energy and direct current (DC) technologies, I would select the following five barriers from the list:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: The higher voltage levels used in DC systems can lead to increased power losses, which could impact efficiency and cost-effectiveness. Ensuring safety and maintaining high-quality electricity is essential for reliable operations.

2. Reduced reliability in DC devices: Some DC components may not be as robust or reliable as their AC counterparts due to differences in design and manufacturing processes. Improving the reliability of DC devices is crucial for widespread adoption.

3. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: Demonstrating compelling reasons for choosing DC over AC, such as reduced power losses, simpler system designs, or enhanced integration of renewable energy sources, is necessary to drive wider acceptance and investment in DC technologies.

4. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): Establishing clear guidelines for net metering, utility involvement, and standardization is essential to ensure fair compensation for excess electricity generated by DC systems and to promote interoperability between different systems.

5. Lack of pilot projects: Real-world demonstrations of DC technologies can help address technical challenges, build confidence in the technology, and showcase its benefits to stakeholders. Increasing the number of successful pilot projects is essential for advancing the adoption of DC power systems.
