Based on the context provided, here are five barriers that I would select for the Shift2DC project:

1. Power losses, quality, and safety issues: Direct current (DC) systems have lower power losses compared to alternating current (AC) systems. However, ensuring quality and safety in DC applications can be challenging due to factors like voltage levels and system design.

2. Lack of use-cases in which DC is advantageous: While there are several benefits to using DC technologies, it's essential to identify and demonstrate clear advantages in various sectors, such as ports, industry, data centers, and buildings, to encourage widespread adoption.

3. Uncertain utility interaction (net metering, utility ownership, and agreed standards): Clear guidelines and standardized protocols for net metering, utility ownership, and DC system standards are necessary to facilitate seamless integration of DC technologies into the existing energy infrastructure.

4. Lack of pilot projects: Demonstrating successful DC solutions through pilot projects can help build confidence in the technology and provide valuable insights for further development and optimization.

5. Incompatibility of DC systems components: Ensuring compatibility among various DC system components, such as solar panels, energy storage devices, and DC microgrids, is crucial to ensure smooth operation and minimizing potential issues during integration.
