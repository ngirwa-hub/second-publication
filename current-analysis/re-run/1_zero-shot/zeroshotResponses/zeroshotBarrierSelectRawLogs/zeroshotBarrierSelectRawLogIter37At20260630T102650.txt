These barriers are significant challenges to the widespread adoption of direct current (DC) technologies. Power losses, quality, and safety issues can impact the efficiency and reliability of DC systems. Reduced reliability in DC devices can lead to operational problems and increased maintenance costs. Uncertain utility interaction can create obstacles for DC integration into the existing grid infrastructure, while a lack of pilot projects can hinder the development and demonstration of innovative DC solutions. Lastly, high costs of DC solutions can make them less competitive compared to traditional alternating current (AC) technologies, which may deter potential investors and stakeholders.
